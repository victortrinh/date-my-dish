Here's the appetizer that starts arguments, the good kind. "Wait, this isn't real calamari?" is the most common reaction, usually said mid-chew with a look of genuine confusion. Golden, shatteringly crisp rings with a tender, meaty interior, fried in a seasoned oregano-cayenne batter. The secret? King oyster mushrooms. Their dense stems slice into perfect rounds, soak up oceanic flavor from kombu water, and fry up so convincingly that even the skeptics reach for seconds. Thirty minutes, no seafood, and a whole lot of swagger.

Not every mushroom can pull this off. Buttons are too soft. Shiitakes are too thin. King oyster mushrooms have thick, cylindrical stems with a firm, almost rubbery texture that survives slicing, soaking, battering, and deep frying without falling apart. Bite through the crispy shell and you hit that same slightly chewy resistance you expect from real calamari. It's uncanny.

The flavor is intentionally mild, which is exactly the point. Traditional calamari isn't about strong fish flavor either. It's about texture and batter. The kombu water soak adds just enough oceanic undertone to bridge the gap between mushroom and seafood, giving your brain that final piece of sensory information to complete the illusion. Your date doesn't need to know the trick. They just need to taste it.

## The Kombu Water Soak

This is the step that separates "fried mushroom rings" from "vegan calamari that fools people." Kombu is dried seaweed packed with glutamic acid, the compound behind umami. Soaking the mushroom rings for 15 to 20 minutes infuses them with a subtle briny depth that plain water can't replicate. It's a quiet power move.

Making kombu water is laughably easy: drop a four-inch strip of dried kombu into a cup and a half of room-temperature water. Wait 30 minutes. The water turns slightly yellow and develops a faint sea smell. That's your flavor bath. Skip this step and you'll have tasty fried mushrooms. Keep it and you'll have something that genuinely makes people pause and question reality.

## Building the Perfect Batter

This batter draws from Mediterranean frying tradition, and every ingredient earns its spot. Flour provides structure. Cornstarch delivers that shatteringly crispy exterior that stays crunchy even as things cool down. Baking powder creates tiny air pockets that keep the coating light instead of heavy and doughy.

The seasoning is simple on purpose. Dried oregano nods to classic Italian calamari. Black pepper brings warmth. Cayenne adds just enough heat to make each bite interesting without steamrolling the delicate mushroom underneath. Together, they taste convincingly like something you'd order at a seaside restaurant, and that's precisely the point.

When mixing, add water gradually. You're aiming for pancake-batter consistency: thick enough to coat a spoon, thin enough to drip off slowly. Too thick and you get a doughy shell. Too thin and the coating slides off during frying. Take your time here. The reward is a perfectly even golden crust that shatters on contact.

## Frying Technique That Delivers Results

Oil temperature is the difference between crispy rings and greasy disappointments. At 350°F, the batter sets on contact, sealing in moisture and creating that satisfying crunch. Too cool and the batter absorbs oil. Too hot and the outside burns while the inside stays raw. Use a thermometer. No shame in precision.

Lower the rings gently and use a wooden spoon to separate any clingers in the first few seconds. Then leave them alone. Two minutes undisturbed, one flip, four to five minutes total per batch. That's it.

And for the love of good food, fry in two batches. Overcrowding drops the oil temperature and gives you pale, soggy rings instead of golden, crispy ones. Patience here is the difference between an appetizer people remember and one they forget.

## Serving and Presentation

Paper towels first to catch excess oil. Then a generous squeeze of lemon, right out of the fryer, while everything's still glistening and hot. That acid brightens the flavor and cuts through the richness in a single move. A scattering of chopped parsley on top for color and a fresh herbal finish.

For dipping, marinara is the classic, since the oregano in the batter practically demands it. Garlic aioli adds richness. Vegan tartar sauce works beautifully. Sriracha mayo or sweet chili if you want heat. Stack the rings on a platter with lemon wedges and sauce on the side for an [appetizer](/en/recipes/category/appetizer/) that starts any dinner with serious confidence.

## Making It a Complete Meal

This vegan calamari shines alone, but it plays well with others too. Pair it with our [Brussels sprouts salad](/en/recipes/brussels-sprouts-salad/) for a crispy-meets-fresh contrast that covers every texture. That's a three-course menu that happens to be entirely plant-based, and nobody will feel like they're missing a thing.

For casual gatherings, double or triple the batch and serve family-style on a big platter. Fair warning: these disappear fast. The rings are best within minutes of frying, so if you're feeding a crowd, fry in waves and bring fresh batches to the table as they finish. The sizzle alone will keep people at the table.
