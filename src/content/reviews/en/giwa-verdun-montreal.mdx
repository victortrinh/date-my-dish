Giwa is the kind of place you pick when you want Korean food that feels a little more polished than your usual spot, but you still want dinner to feel easy. The plates show up looking sharp, the technique is there, and the menu plays with Korean classics in a way that feels modern without getting weird about it.

One honest note going in: the flavors lean refined, not loud. If you're chasing maximum heat, deep fermentation funk, or the kind of punch you expect from a big bold Korean meal, you might leave wishing it pushed further. But if you want a pretty, low-pressure night where the food still feels thoughtful, Giwa is a really solid date move.

## The Vibe

Inside, it feels like a classic casual Korean restaurant: welcoming, straightforward, and not pretending to be fine dining. That's exactly why it works for a date. You can focus on your person, not on performing restaurant etiquette.

We sat on the terrasse, and if the weather is good, I'd recommend doing the same. It instantly turns dinner into a proper Montreal summer night. The noise level sits at low to medium, closer to a neighborhood dinner than a party room. Lighting is natural and easy. You can hold a real conversation without leaning across the table.

Best seats: terrasse for the summer date-night vibe, inside if the season turns or you want a more contained atmosphere. Casual dress works fine either way.

## What to Order

This is a sharing spot. Order in layers so the table keeps changing while you talk.

For two: two appetizers, one main, one seasonal dish, one sot bap, and dessert. And here's the thing: don't skip the sot bap, even if you're not usually a sot bap person. Giwa won a bibimbap competition at the Montreal Korean Festival 2026, judged by Edward Lee. That's a pretty strong signal that this is the plate the kitchen is most proud of. I didn't order it, and I regret it.

The order logic that makes it feel like a date rather than a menu-sweep: start with something light and bright, follow with one comforting classic, and add one "twist" dish from the seasonal menu. That structure keeps the energy moving without rushing anyone.

## The Dishes

The strongest thing Giwa does is how the food looks. Clean, modern, genuinely photo-friendly, and the technique shows in the details. The seasonal menu keeps things from feeling like a greatest-hits rotation.

**Gimbap:** seaweed roll with fish of the moment, cucumber, avocado, and a gochujang vinaigrette. Fresh, lightly sweet, gently spicy, and easy to share. Great first bite.

**Baechu (seasonal):** charred napa cabbage glazed with doenjang, served over perilla cream with cucumber and fresh herbs. This was the most interesting dish of the night: smoky-sweet cabbage, savory doenjang funk, and a cooling herbal finish from the perilla and cucumber. It's the dish that most clearly shows the chef's point of view, and it's the best reason to come back when the seasonal menu rotates.

**Desserts:** three options, three different vibes. The doenjang crème brûlée is clean and properly executed, with a sweet-salty-fermented edge that's interesting but not quite my thing. The Basque corn cheesecake with torched corn and corn ice cream is creamy and toasty, though the popcorn topping tasted a bit stale. The winner is the Hodumaru: gochujang chocolate mousse, chili-spiked chantilly, candied walnuts, and maple ice cream. Chocolate-forward with a warm chili tingle, a crunchy-nutty top, and maple creaminess to pull everything together. Order that one.

We had the Somaek (fresh soju and Origine lager: crisp, clean, and light) and the Omija (gin, five-flavor berry syrup, lemon: bright, fruity-tart, and easy to drink). Both work well as openers while you settle in and figure out what to share.

Date tip: one drink each to start, then switch to water or tea. Keeps the night light and the conversation going without the check becoming the story.

## The Real Cost

About $90 CAD per person based on our meal. That puts Giwa solidly in the "nice night out without full fine-dining pressure" range.

Worth it if you care about technique and plating and want Korean flavors that feel approachable. If budget matters: skip the second round of drinks and add one more dish to share instead. More food, better food memories, and you'll still feel like you got something for your money.

## Before and After

Verdun is easy to build a full night around.

Before dinner: a golden-hour walk along the Verdun waterfront at Parc Arthur-Therrien, or a slow stroll down Promenade Wellington to window-shop and arrive hungry. If you're coming from downtown, make the trip to Verdun part of the date. Meet there and walk Wellington together.

After dinner: keep it going on Wellington (the "keep talking" walk) or head back down to the waterfront and sit for a few minutes. Quiet, low-pressure, no reservation needed.

The full mini-plan: meet in Verdun, waterfront walk at Parc Arthur-Therrien, terrasse at Giwa, post-dinner stroll on Wellington.

## Is It a Date Spot?

For a first date: 4/5. Relaxed vibe, not too expensive-feeling, easy to talk. The terrasse helps.

For an anniversary: 3/5. More cute and casual than big occasion. A solid choice if Korean food is the whole point of the night, but not the move for a landmark dinner.

For impressing someone who cares about food: 3/5. The plating and technique do a lot of the work, even if the flavors play it a little safe.

Giwa scores 7 out of 10 as a date-night restaurant. It's not a jaw-drop moment, and it won't deliver the flavor intensity you might expect from Korean food. But when you want a relaxed Montreal night, pretty plates, and a terrasse in summer with a walk built in after, it delivers that cleanly and easily.

If you want to bring some of that Korean-inspired energy home, our [gochujang kimchi seafood bucatini](/en/recipes/gochujang-kimchi-seafood-bucatini/) or [lamb meatballs with gochujang glaze](/en/recipes/lamb-meatballs-gochujang-glaze/) carry similar bold-meets-technique energy. But when you want someone else to do the cooking, Giwa on Wellington is worth the metro ride.
