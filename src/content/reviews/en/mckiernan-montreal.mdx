McKiernan is my pick when I want brunch-lunch energy in Montreal without the fuss. It sits right by the Lachine Canal in the Sud-Ouest, serves rotisserie comfort food built for sharing, and lets you eat until you're genuinely happy. Coffee first, plates for the table, and nowhere to be afterward except the canal path. If you're planning a daytime date or a lazy catch-up with friends, this is the spot.

## The vibe

The space is a converted industrial room with high ceilings, big windows, and natural light that makes everything feel warm and open. It's bright without being harsh, and the noise level stays at a comfortable buzz. You can hold a conversation without leaning in, which counts for a lot on a date.

The crowd is a mix of couples, friend groups, and families who all seem to be in a very good mood. Staff are casual and genuinely friendly. Nobody's rushing you, nobody's hovering. Best seats are along the windows if you can get them, but honestly the whole room works. It's the kind of place where you show up slightly underdressed and feel completely at home.

## What we ordered

McKiernan is built for sharing, and you should order that way. Two to three plates for two people is the sweet spot. We went a bit over that and rolled out the door, which, honestly, was also fine.

The devilled Brussels sprouts came out first. Crispy-edged, salty-sweet, and gone before anything else on the table. If you've ever been skeptical about Brussels sprouts, this dish will fix that permanently. They're the kind of opener that sets the tone for the whole meal.

The zesty Italian beef tartare was next. It leans Italian in its seasoning, comes with endive leaves for scooping, and has enough crunch and parmesan on top to keep every bite interesting. Light, fresh, and a nice counterpoint before the heavier dishes.

**Sharing tip:** two to three plates total for two people. **What I'd do differently next time:** keep it tighter. The room tempts you into ordering like you're settling in for a feast, and it's easy to over-order. I'd skip one of the mains and leave more room for the donuts.

## Drinks and pacing

McKiernan is a coffee-first brunch spot. The americano is solid and well-brewed. Not specialty pour-over territory, but reliable and satisfying. If you're the kind of person who needs two cups before becoming conversational, this is a safe landing. The espresso is good too, if you prefer to keep it short.

They have a short brunch cocktail list if you're in that mood, but honestly, the food here is the star. You don't need a Bloody Mary to make it interesting. Save the drinks budget for another meal and let the cooking do the work.

Service is friendly and unpretentious. The staff reads the room well: attentive when you need something, invisible when you don't. Pacing felt smooth and steady. Plates landed at a comfortable rhythm, nobody rushed our coffee, and there was zero pressure to turn the table. It's the kind of service that makes you stay longer than you planned.

## The real cost

Expect $30 to $55 per person depending on how many plates you share and whether you go for extras. You can keep it lighter at $20 to $35 with coffee and one main each, but you'd be missing the point. The sharing is where the fun lives.

For what you get, the room, the location, the quality of the cooking, the price is honest. This isn't a place that charges a premium for the neighbourhood or the aesthetic. The food earns its price tag on its own.

## Is it a date spot?

First date: 4 out of 5. Brunch keeps things low-pressure and easy. The sharing format gives you something to talk about, and you can follow up with a walk along the canal. It's casual enough that nobody feels overdressed or underdressed.

Friends catch-up: 5 out of 5. This is where McKiernan really shines. The big table, the shared plates, the relaxed pacing. Perfect for a group of three or four who want to eat well and linger.

Impressing out-of-towners: 4 out of 5. The canal-side location gives it a sense of place, and the food is distinctly Montreal without being tourist-bait. It tells visitors you know where to eat.

Reserve on weekends, especially when the sun is out. Every brunch-loving Montrealer within a five-kilometre radius remembers McKiernan exists the moment it hits 15 degrees. Peak hours on Saturday and Sunday fill fast. Weekday brunch is calmer and walk-in friendly. If you can swing a Friday morning, that's the move: full menu, no wait, and the room feels almost like a weekday coffee shop before it shifts into weekend mode.

The real pro tip: eat brunch, then walk the Lachine Canal. It's five minutes from the restaurant, and it turns a good meal into a full morning. McKiernan scores 8 out of 10 for brunch dates. Go here if you want honest, satisfying food in a room that feels good, with a built-in second act by the water. Skip it if you're looking for a quiet, intimate setting or polished fine dining. That's not what this place does. What it does is feed you well, keep the energy up, and send you off happy. Bring someone you want to spend a lazy morning with. Order too much. Walk it off by the canal. That's the whole playbook, and it works every time.

**Hours:** Monday to Friday 11:00 AM to 10:30 PM. Saturday and Sunday 10:00 AM to 10:30 PM (brunch runs until 3:00 PM).
**Getting there:** Best by bike or rideshare. Street parking exists but varies, especially on sunny weekends.
**Dietary note:** The menu usually includes vegetarian-friendly small plates. Ask your server about current options.
