Some brunch spots are precious about it. Tiny portions, a 45-minute wait for a single poached egg, and a vibe that says "we know you need this more than we need you." McKiernan is not that. It's a big, warm, industrial-feeling room near the Lachine Canal that puts rotisserie comfort food on the table and lets you eat until you're genuinely happy. Coffee first, plates to share, and nowhere to be afterward except the canal path.

## The Vibe

McKiernan sits in the Sud-Ouest, just off the Lachine Canal, in a space that feels like a converted warehouse in the best way. High ceilings, big windows, natural light pouring in. The room is large enough that you don't feel stacked on top of other tables, which is rare for a popular Montreal brunch spot. There's an energy here that runs on coffee and good food rather than cocktail-fuelled chaos.

It's the kind of place where you show up slightly underdressed and feel completely at home. The crowd is a mix of couples, friend groups, and families who all seem to be in a very good mood. Staff are casual, quick, and genuinely friendly. Nobody's rushing you, nobody's hovering. You order, you eat, you stay as long as you want.

## What to Order

McKiernan is built for sharing, and you should order that way. Two to three plates for two people is the sweet spot. More than that and you'll be rolling out the door, which, honestly, is also fine.

Start with the devilled Brussels sprouts. Crispy-edged, salty-sweet, and they disappear faster than anything else on the table. If you've ever been skeptical about Brussels sprouts, this dish will fix that permanently. The beef tartare is another strong opener. It's zesty, Italian-leaning in its seasoning, and comes with enough crunch on top to keep every bite interesting.

The bomba schnitzel is the headliner. Golden, crunchy breading with a kick of bomba heat and enough acidity to cut through the richness. It's aggressively satisfying in the way that only fried, well-seasoned food can be. This is the dish you'll think about on the walk home. The honey garlic spare ribs are its perfect counterpart: sticky, glossy, sweet on the front end and savory on the finish.

For dessert, the homemade donuts. Warm, fluffy, dusted, and dangerously easy to keep eating. They're the kind of thing you order "for the table" and then quietly eat most of yourself. No judgment here.

## The Coffee

McKiernan is a coffee-first brunch spot, and they do it well. It's not specialty pour-over territory, but the coffee is solid, well-brewed, and refillable in spirit if not in policy. If you're the kind of person who needs two cups before they become conversational, this is a safe landing.

They have a short cocktail list for brunch if you're in that mood, but honestly, the food here is the star. You don't need a Bloody Mary to make it interesting. Save the drinks budget for another meal and let the cooking do the work.

## The Real Cost

McKiernan is one of the better-value brunch experiences in Montreal. Expect $30 to $55 per person depending on how many plates you share and whether you go for extras. You can keep it tighter at $20 to $35 with coffee and one main each, but you'd be missing the point. The sharing is where the fun lives.

For what you get, the room, the location, the quality of the cooking, the price is honest. This isn't a place that charges a premium for the neighbourhood or the aesthetic. The food earns its price tag on its own. If you're looking for a brunch that feels like an occasion without costing like one, McKiernan lands right in that pocket.

## Booking and Timing

Reserve on weekends. Especially when the sun is out. McKiernan sits near the Lachine Canal, and when the weather turns nice, every brunch-loving Montrealer within a five-kilometre radius remembers it exists. Peak hours on Saturday and Sunday fill fast.

Weekday brunch is calmer and walk-in friendly. If you can swing a Friday morning, that's the move: full menu, no wait, and the room has a relaxed, almost weekday-coffee-shop feeling before it shifts into weekend mode.

Here's the real pro tip: eat brunch, then walk the Lachine Canal. It's five minutes from the restaurant, and it turns a good meal into a full morning. If you're on a date, that post-brunch walk is where the real conversation happens. ## Bottom Line

McKiernan is an 8 out of 10 for brunch dates. The room is great, the food is honest and satisfying, and the location by the canal gives you a built-in second act. It's not trying to be fancy. It's trying to feed you well in a space that feels good, and it nails both.

Bring someone you want to spend a lazy morning with. Order too much food. Walk it off by the water. That's the whole playbook, and it works every time.
