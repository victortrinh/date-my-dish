Île Flottante is a date-night spot for people who want to impress without looking like they're trying to impress.

It's the kind of place where you can show up looking good, sit down, and let the tasting menu carry the night while you focus on the real goal: good conversation and a little tension in the best way. Michelin-featured, which fits. This is a restaurant with clear conviction about what it's doing.

My honest take: the technique and execution are excellent, and service is smooth. But for the price, I expected at least one dish that would sit with me a week later. I had a really nice night. I just didn't leave fully obsessed.

Île Flottante replaced the beloved Les Deux Singes de Montarvie on Rue Saint-Viateur in Mile End. Les Deux Singes opened in 2011 and built a real following. Toward the end of 2016, owners Nada Abou Younes and chef Sean Murray Smith knew it was time to evolve. When the adjacent space became available, they expanded, renovated, and reimagined the whole concept, including the name.

The name is personal and a little symbolic: it's Sean's favorite dessert, but it also nods to Montreal as an island. There's an Expo 67 connection (Sean's father worked there), and the name quietly references Île Sainte-Hélène. It's a "this is Montréal" restaurant identity, not just a menu.

## How the Menu Works

This is a chef-driven tasting menu. You're not choosing from a long list of options. The kitchen decides the sequence for the night, and you go with it.

That can be fun if you like discovery, but it also raises the bar. When you're paying for a tasting menu, you're paying for point of view, pacing, and memorability, not just correct cooking.

Practical tip: tell them allergies and real dislikes early. It helps the kitchen steer you away from a hard no, and it makes the whole flow feel more tailored.

## The Food: My Honest Take

From a technical standpoint, it's good. Ingredients are fresh and handled properly. Technique is controlled and confident. Execution is clean, precise, and consistent throughout.

Where it didn't fully land for me was the flavor identity relative to the price. Some combinations felt a little mismatched, and I didn't get that "this is the chef's profile" feeling. At this level, I'm looking for at least one dish that creates a memory. For me, the meal stayed in the "very good" lane, but didn't break into "I need to bring someone here."

## Service and Drinks

Service was genuinely good: polished, smooth, and professional. It's the kind of service that makes the meal feel calm. Good timing, no awkward hovering, and it supports the "take your time" vibe perfectly.

For drinks, I'd treat the program here as something that supports the meal rather than the main attraction. The best move for a date is usually one drink at the start to settle in, then keep it simple so you stay present. If you like asking for guidance, this is a good place to do it. Just say what you normally like (bright, bitter, boozy, fruity) and let them steer you.

## The Real Cost

The tasting menu is $130 per person before drinks, tax, and tip. With one cocktail, plan for around $170 per person. For two people, that's roughly $300 to $350 all in.

Worth it when the occasion calls for it. If you're going for an anniversary or a night that already has some weight, that math makes sense. If you're not sure it's that kind of night, the math might not.

## Before and After

Mile End makes this easy. You can turn the whole evening into a proper date without doing anything complicated.

**Before dinner:** Wander Rue Saint-Viateur Ouest for 10 to 15 minutes and arrive relaxed. Do the bagel debate at St-Viateur Bagel or Fairmount Bagel (grab one, argue about which is better). Or grab a coffee at Café Olimpico for some classic Mile End energy. Bar Waverly works well for a pre-dinner drink if you want to start the night before you sit down.

**After dinner:** A slow Mile End walk is the best "keep talking" move. Ice cream at Kem CoBa is an easy dessert extension without committing to a second full sit-down. If you want one more drink, Bar Waverly for something lively, Le Kabinet or Datcha for cocktails and later-night energy, or Dieu du Ciel! if you both want a casual beer to close the night.

## Is It a Date Spot?

For a first date, probably not. A long tasting menu can feel like a commitment before you know if the chemistry is there, and you're locked in for two and a half hours no matter what. If you're looking for a lower-stakes, conversation-friendly option, check out our [Othym review](/en/reviews/othym-montreal/) for a quieter BYOB spot that works better early on.

For an anniversary, it earns its keep. The tasting menu gives the night a shape: a beginning, a middle, somewhere you're clearly going together. The room is calm enough to stay in your own world for the whole meal. That pace, in that room, at that level of service, adds up to a night that feels like you planned it together, which is exactly what an anniversary should feel like.

If you're going with someone who cares about food and you both want to compare notes course by course, this works well too. There's something to discuss between every plate, and the kitchen gives you enough to react to.

If you want to cook something with that same grown-up, "we took our time" energy at home, our [beef ragu pappardelle](/en/recipes/beef-ragu-pappardelle/) or [pork osso buco](/en/recipes/pork-osso-buco/) carry similar slow, layered vibes. But when you want someone else to do the work, Île Flottante is a strong call for the right occasion.

Île Flottante scores 7.5 out of 10 as a date-night restaurant. Don't come expecting a dish that stops the table. Come when you want calm, polished, and grown-up, and let the tasting menu carry the conversation.
