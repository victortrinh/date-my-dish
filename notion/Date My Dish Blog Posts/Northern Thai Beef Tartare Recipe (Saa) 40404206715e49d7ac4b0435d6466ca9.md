## Ingredients for Northern Thai Beef Tartare

**For the Tartare:**

- 7 ounces (200 g) AAA beef striploin steak, thinly sliced against the grain
- ¼ cup laab powder
- 2 tbsp garlic oil
- 3 tbsp Thai fish sauce
- 1 tbsp fresh lime juice
- ¼ cup packed coarsely chopped fresh cilantro leaves and stems
- ⅓ cup packed coarsely chopped fresh sawtooth coriander
- ¼ cup packed coarsely chopped fresh mint
- ¼ cup packed coarsely chopped fresh finger mint
- 2 green onions, thinly sliced
- ¼ cup crispy shallots
- ⅓ cup garlic chips (without the oil)

**For Serving:**

- 1 head Boston lettuce, leaves separated (about 20 leaves)
- 1 English cucumber, sliced (optional)
- Thai basil leaves (optional)

**Ingredient Notes:**

AAA beef striploin is essential. You're eating this raw, so quality matters. Buy from a trusted butcher and use it the same day.

Laab powder is toasted rice powder with spices. You can make it or buy it at Thai grocery stores.

Garlic oil adds richness and prevents the meat from drying out. Make your own by frying garlic in oil.

Sawtooth coriander has a unique flavor. Regular cilantro works if you can't find it.

Finger mint (Vietnamese mint) has a peppery taste. Regular mint works in a pinch.

Crispy shallots add texture. Find them at Asian markets or make your own.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Prepare the Beef**

Make sure your beef is very fresh and high quality. Slice it thinly against the grain. The thinner the slices, the better the texture. Place in a large mixing bowl.

**Step 2: Mix the Tartare**

Add the laab powder and garlic oil to the beef and mix well. The laab powder should coat the beef evenly. Add the fish sauce, lime juice, cilantro, sawtooth coriander, mint, finger mint, and green onions. Toss everything together until well combined. The beef should be fully coated with the herbs and seasonings.

**Step 3: Plate and Garnish**

Transfer the beef tartare to a large serving plate. Sprinkle the crispy shallots and garlic chips on top for texture and extra flavor.

**Step 4: Prepare the Lettuce Cups**

Separate the Boston lettuce leaves and arrange them on a serving plate. If using, serve sliced cucumber and Thai basil leaves on the side.

**Step 5: Serve**

To eat, spoon the beef tartare onto a lettuce leaf, wrap it up, and eat it like a taco. Add cucumber or Thai basil for extra crunch and flavor. Serve immediately. Pairs perfectly with rice whiskey or cold beer.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Northern Thai Beef Tartare

**Use the Freshest Beef Possible**

This is raw beef. Buy from a trusted butcher and use it the same day. Look for AAA or Prime grade striploin with good marbling.

**Slice Against the Grain**

Slicing against the grain makes the beef tender. Slicing with the grain makes it chewy.

**Don't Skip the Laab Powder**

The toasted rice powder is what makes this Northern Thai. Without it, it's just beef salad.

**Add the Crispy Elements Last**

Crispy shallots and garlic chips get soggy fast. Add them right before serving.

**Serve Immediately**

This dish is best eaten fresh. The lime juice will start to "cook" the beef if it sits too long.

## FAQs About Northern Thai Beef Tartare

**Is it safe to eat raw beef?**

Yes, if you use high-quality, fresh beef from a trusted source. Buy from a reputable butcher and use it the same day.

**Can I use a different cut of beef?**

Striploin is traditional, but you can use tenderloin or sirloin. Avoid tough cuts like chuck.

**What if I can't find laab powder?**

Make your own by toasting sticky rice until golden, then grinding it with coriander seeds, cumin, and dried chilies.

**How long can this sit before serving?**

Make it right before serving. The lime juice will start to cure the beef if it sits longer than 30 minutes.

**Can I make this less spicy?**

Reduce the amount of laab powder or use a milder version. The fresh herbs are essential, though.
