## Ingredients for Mapo Tofu

**Main Ingredients:**

- 230 g soft silken tofu (8 oz)
- 5 tbsp Sichuan peppercorns, freshly ground
- 35 g ground pork (1 1/4 oz)
- 2-3 cloves garlic, minced
- 5 tbsp Sichuan chili bean paste (doubanjiang)
- 1 tbsp cayenne pepper
- 75 ml chicken stock (1/3 cup)

**For the Sauce:**

- 1 tsp Shaoxing cooking wine
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- 1 tsp sugar
- 1/2 tsp Chinese black vinegar
- 1 tsp cornstarch mixed with 1 tbsp water
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil

**Ingredient Notes:**

Soft silken tofu is essential. It has that custard-like texture that makes this dish so luxurious. Don't use firm tofu.

Sichuan chili bean paste (doubanjiang) is the soul of this dish. Look for it at Asian groceries in Montreal like Marché C&T or Kim Phat. The fermented, funky, spicy flavor is irreplaceable.

Fresh Sichuan peppercorns make a huge difference. Toast them yourself for maximum numbing power.

Shaoxing wine adds depth. You can find it at most Asian markets, or substitute with dry sherry in a pinch.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Toast the Sichuan Peppercorns**

In a dry pan over medium-low heat, toast your freshly ground Sichuan peppercorns without any oil. You'll know they're done when they become aromatic and start leaving small oil spots on the pan. This usually takes 2-3 minutes. Set aside.

**Step 2: Prepare the Tofu**

Cut your tofu into small cubes, about 1/2 inch each. Bring a pot of lightly salted water to a bare simmer. Add your tofu cubes and let them simmer for 2-3 minutes. This helps firm them up slightly and seasons them. Remove the pot from heat but keep the tofu soaking in the hot water until you're ready to use it.

**Step 3: Fry the Ground Pork**

Here's the key technique: start with cold oil. Add your ground pork to a wok or large pan with cold oil. Then gradually increase the heat to medium-high. This renders out the fat from the pork and gets it crispy. Fry for a few minutes until the pork is browned and crispy around the edges.

**Step 4: Fry the Chili Bean Paste**

Add the chili bean paste to the pork and fry over medium heat for 2-3 minutes. This is crucial. Frying the doubanjiang brings out its complex flavors and turns the oil bright red. Your kitchen will smell incredible.

**Step 5: Add Garlic and Cayenne**

Add your minced garlic and cayenne pepper. Fry for about 1 minute until the garlic is fragrant. Don't let it burn.

**Step 6: Add Stock and Seasonings**

Pour in the chicken stock, then add the soy sauce, Shaoxing wine, and sugar. Stir everything together and let it simmer for a few minutes. The liquid should reduce slightly and the flavors will concentrate.

**Step 7: Add the Tofu**

Drain your tofu well. Make sure there's no water clinging to it. Gently add the tofu to your pan.

**Step 8: Simmer and Reduce**

Increase the heat to medium-high to get a vigorous simmer. Gently push the tofu around with your spatula, being careful not to break it up. Let it simmer in the sauce as the liquid reduces. After about 3 minutes, the liquid should be bubbling rapidly and look like a thin sauce.

**Step 9: Add Peppercorns and Vinegar**

Add your toasted Sichuan peppercorns and 1/2 tsp of black vinegar. Stir gently and cook for about 30 seconds. The peppercorns will create that signature numbing sensation.

**Step 10: Thicken the Sauce**

Add your cornstarch slurry (1 tsp cornstarch mixed with 1 tbsp water) to thicken the sauce. Stir gently. The sauce should coat the tofu and become glossy. Turn off the heat.

**Step 11: Finish with Sesame Oil**

Drizzle a little toasted sesame oil over the top and give it one final gentle stir. Serve immediately over steamed rice.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Mapo Tofu

**Toast Your Own Peppercorns**

Pre-ground Sichuan peppercorns lose their numbing power quickly. Buy whole peppercorns, toast them, and grind them fresh for maximum málà effect.

**Don't Skip Frying the Doubanjiang**

This step develops the deep, complex flavors. The oil should turn bright red and smell incredibly fragrant.

**Be Gentle with the Tofu**

Silken tofu breaks easily. Push it around gently with your spatula rather than stirring aggressively.

**Start with Cold Oil for the Pork**

This renders out the fat properly and gives you crispier, more flavorful pork.

**Adjust the Heat Level**

If 1 tbsp of cayenne is too much, start with 1 tsp and work your way up. You can always add more heat, but you can't take it away.

## FAQs About Mapo Tofu

**What does málà mean?**

Málà means "numbing and spicy." Má is the numbing sensation from Sichuan peppercorns, and là is the heat from chilis. Together they create the signature flavor profile of Sichuan cuisine.

**Can I make this vegetarian?**

Yes. Skip the pork and use mushrooms (shiitake work great) for texture, and use vegetable stock instead of chicken stock. The doubanjiang and Sichuan peppercorns still give you all that flavor.

**Why is my tofu falling apart?**

You might be using firm tofu instead of soft silken tofu, or stirring too aggressively. Be gentle and push the tofu around rather than stirring.

**Can I make this less spicy?**

Reduce the cayenne pepper to 1 tsp instead of 1 tbsp. You can also use less chili bean paste, but that will affect the overall flavor since doubanjiang is essential to the dish.

**Where can I find doubanjiang in Montreal?**

Marché C&T on Boulevard Saint-Laurent, Kim Phat locations across the city, or Épicerie Coréenne on Rue Sainte-Catherine Ouest all carry it.

**How do I store leftovers?**

Mapo tofu keeps in the fridge for 2-3 days. The tofu will absorb more flavor over time. Reheat gently on the stove with a splash of water or stock to loosen the sauce.
