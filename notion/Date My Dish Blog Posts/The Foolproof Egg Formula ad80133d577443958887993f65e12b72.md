## The Universal Principles

### Principle 1: Low and Slow

Eggs cook fast. High heat creates rubbery, overcooked results. Low to medium-low heat gives you control.

### Principle 2: Remove Before Done

Carryover cooking continues after you remove eggs from heat. Pull them slightly underdone.

### Principle 3: Fresh Matters

Fresh eggs (under 2 weeks old) have firmer whites and taller yolks. They fry and poach better.

### Principle 4: Room Temperature

Cold eggs straight from fridge cook unevenly. Let sit 10 minutes before cooking.

## Scrambled Eggs (The French Method)

Creamy, custard-like texture that shows technique.

### Ingredients (per person)

- 3 eggs
- 1 tablespoon butter
- Salt and pepper
- Optional: 1 tablespoon cream or crème fraîche

### Method

**Step 1: Whisk eggs thoroughly**

Break up proteins completely. No streaks of white.

**Step 2: Heat pan on medium-low**

Add butter, let melt but not brown.

**Step 3: Add eggs**

Pour in, let sit 10 seconds.

**Step 4: Stir constantly with silicone spatula**

Figure-eight pattern, scraping bottom and sides. 3-5 minutes total.

**Step 5: Remove at soft curds**

Still slightly wet and glossy. They'll continue cooking.

**Step 6: Serve immediately**

Add cream or butter off heat if desired. Season.

**Result:** Silky, creamy eggs with small, soft curds.

## Fried Eggs (Crispy Edges, Runny Yolk)

Classic preparation that requires heat control.

### Method

**Step 1: Heat pan medium-high**

Add 1-2 tablespoons butter or oil.

**Step 2: Crack eggs into small bowl first**

Ensures no shell pieces, easier to slide into pan.

**Step 3: Slide eggs into hot fat**

Should sizzle immediately.

**Step 4: Let cook undisturbed (2-3 minutes)**

Whites set, edges get crispy and brown.

**Step 5: Baste with hot fat or cover briefly**

For over-easy: flip gently for 30 seconds.

For sunny-side up: baste top with hot butter or cover 30 seconds to set white.

**Step 6: Season and serve**

Salt and pepper after cooking.

**Target:** White fully set, yolk warm and runny.

## Poached Eggs (Elegant, Difficult to Master)

Impressive technique that shows skill.

### Method

**Step 1: Fresh eggs only**

Old eggs spread in water. Fresh eggs hold together.

**Step 2: Bring water to bare simmer**

Tiny bubbles, not rolling boil. Add 1 tablespoon vinegar (helps whites coagulate).

**Step 3: Create gentle whirlpool**

Stir water in circle with spoon.

**Step 4: Crack egg into small bowl**

Slide into center of whirlpool gently.

**Step 5: Cook 3-4 minutes**

White fully set, yolk soft.

**Step 6: Remove with slotted spoon**

Drain on paper towel briefly.

**Tip:** Practice this alone first. It takes attempts to master.

## Soft-Boiled Eggs (Jammy Yolk)

Consistent, foolproof, impressive presentation.

### Method

**Step 1: Room temperature eggs**

Cold eggs crack in boiling water.

**Step 2: Boiling water**

Full rolling boil in pot.

**Step 3: Lower eggs gently**

Use spoon or egg basket.

**Step 4: Set timer**

- 6 minutes: Liquid yolk, set white
- 7 minutes: Jammy, custard-like yolk (ideal)
- 8 minutes: Fully set but still soft

**Step 5: Ice bath immediately**

Stop cooking process.

**Step 6: Peel under running water**

Start from wide end where air pocket is.

**Serve:** Halved on toast, in ramen, or alone with salt.

## Omelets (French vs. American)

### French Omelet

**Texture:** Smooth exterior, creamy inside, no browning

**Method:**

- Beat eggs with fork
- Medium heat, butter
- Constantly shake pan while stirring with fork
- Roll onto plate when still slightly wet

**Time:** 60-90 seconds total

**Best for:** Showing technique, elegant brunch

---

### American Omelet

**Texture:** Slightly browned, fully cooked, sturdy

**Method:**

- Beat eggs
- Medium heat, butter
- Let set 30 seconds
- Lift edges, tilt to let uncooked egg flow under
- Add fillings when mostly set
- Fold in half

**Time:** 3-4 minutes

**Best for:** Heartier breakfast, holds fillings better

## Common Mistakes

❌ **High heat**

✅ Medium-low for scrambled, medium for fried. Eggs need gentle heat.

❌ **Overcooking**

✅ Remove before fully set. They continue cooking.

❌ **Stirring scrambled eggs too aggressively**

✅ Gentle, constant motion creates small curds.

❌ **Old eggs for poaching**

✅ Fresh eggs (under 1 week) hold together best.

❌ **Not preheating pan**

✅ Eggs need immediate heat to set properly.

## Pro Tips

### For Scrambled Eggs

- Add cream or butter at the very end, off heat
- Season after cooking (salt draws out moisture)
- Use silicone spatula for gentle stirring

### For Fried Eggs

- Butter browns and adds flavor, oil stays neutral
- Tilt pan to pool fat for basting
- Cover briefly if you don't want to flip

### For Poached Eggs

- Strain raw egg through fine sieve first (removes loose whites)
- Multiple eggs: poach in batches, rewarm in hot water
- Make ahead: poach, store in ice water, reheat when needed

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do I know if eggs are fresh?**

Float test: Fresh eggs sink in water. Old eggs float. Or check the sell-by date (use within 3-4 weeks of laying).

**Q: Why do my scrambled eggs get watery?**

Overcooked. The proteins squeeze out moisture. Remove from heat earlier.

**Q: Can I make poached eggs ahead?**

Yes. Poach, store in ice water in fridge. Reheat 1 minute in simmering water before serving.

**Q: What's the green ring around hard-boiled yolk?**

Overcooked. It's harmless iron sulfide. Use shorter cooking time and ice bath immediately.

**Q: Does adding milk to scrambled eggs help?**

No. It dilutes flavor and makes them watery. Use cream sparingly or none at all.
