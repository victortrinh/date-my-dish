Polenta is one of those versatile dishes you reach for when you don't want rice or pasta. It's made from cornmeal and used across different countries. In Italy, it's a northern staple served with everything from braised meats to seafood. In parts of Eastern Europe, similar cornmeal porridges like Romanian mămăligă are everyday comfort food. Latin American countries have their own versions with dishes like Colombian or Venezuelan arepas made from cornmeal. What makes polenta great is how it adapts to whatever you're serving. It can be creamy and soft as a base for saucy dishes, or cooled and grilled for something with texture. It's simple to make but needs attention while cooking to get it right.

## Ingredients for Creamy Parmesan Polenta

- 4 cups water (1 L)
- 1 tsp fine salt
- 1 cup polenta (170 g)
- 3 tbsp butter (45 g), divided
- 1/2 cup freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese (50 g), plus more for garnish

**Ingredient Notes:**

Polenta is coarsely ground cornmeal. Look for packages labeled "polenta" at Italian groceries in Montreal like Milano or Quincaillerie Dante, or in the Italian section at IGA or Provigo. Don't use fine cornmeal or instant polenta for this recipe.

The grind size affects cooking time. Coarse polenta takes longer but has better texture. Medium grind is the most common and works great.

Freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano makes a huge difference. The pre-grated stuff doesn't melt as smoothly and has less flavor.

Use good butter. It's one of only five ingredients, so quality matters.

Fine salt dissolves better than coarse salt in the water.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Bring Water to a Boil**

In a large, heavy-bottomed saucepan, bring the water and salt to a rolling boil over high heat. The heavy bottom prevents scorching.

**Step 2: Add the Polenta**

Reduce heat to medium. Pour the polenta into the boiling water in a slow, steady stream while whisking constantly with your other hand. This is crucial. If you dump it all in at once, you'll get lumps. Keep whisking until all the polenta is incorporated and there are no lumps visible.

**Step 3: Initial Simmer**

Reduce heat to low and continue whisking often until the polenta starts to thicken, about 5 minutes. At this point it should still be slightly loose and pourable. Don't walk away. Keep whisking every 30 seconds or so.

**Step 4: Long Simmer**

Cover the pot and cook for 30 minutes, whisking every 5 to 6 minutes. Set a timer so you don't forget. Each time you whisk, scrape the bottom and sides of the pot to prevent sticking. As the polenta thickens, it will become too thick to whisk easily. When this happens, switch to a wooden spoon and stir instead.

**Step 5: Check for Doneness**

The polenta is done when the texture is smooth and creamy, and the individual grains are tender, not gritty. Taste a small spoonful to check. If it still feels grainy or raw, cook for another 5 to 10 minutes. Cooking time varies depending on the grind of your polenta.

**Step 6: Add Butter and Cheese**

Turn off the heat. Add 2 tablespoons of the butter and stir gently until it's partially melted and incorporated. Add the 1/2 cup of grated Parmigiano-Reggiano and stir until the cheese has completely melted into the polenta. The polenta should be glossy and rich.

**Step 7: Rest**

Cover the pot and let the polenta stand for 5 minutes. This allows it to thicken slightly and the flavors to meld.

**Step 8: Serve**

Stir the polenta one final time. It should flow like lava, thick but pourable. Transfer to a serving bowl. Top with the remaining 1 tablespoon of butter and about 1 tablespoon of freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano as garnish. Serve immediately while hot and creamy.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Polenta

**Add Polenta Slowly**

This is the number one rule. A slow, steady stream while whisking constantly prevents lumps.

**Whisk and Stir Regularly**

Polenta sticks and burns easily. Set a timer and don't skip your stirring intervals.

**Use a Heavy-Bottomed Pot**

This distributes heat evenly and prevents scorching on the bottom.

**Taste for Doneness**

Don't rely on time alone. The polenta should taste smooth and cooked, not gritty.

**Adjust Consistency**

If your polenta is too thick, whisk in a little hot water. If it's too thin, cook it a bit longer uncovered.

## FAQs About Polenta

**What's the difference between polenta and cornmeal?**

Polenta is a specific type of coarsely ground cornmeal. Regular cornmeal is finer and won't give you the same texture.

**Can I use instant polenta?**

Instant polenta cooks in 5 minutes but doesn't have the same creamy texture or depth of flavor as traditional polenta.

**Why is my polenta lumpy?**

You added the polenta too fast or didn't whisk enough at the beginning. Next time, pour it in a slower, steadier stream while whisking constantly.

**Can I make polenta ahead?**

You can make it a few hours ahead and reheat it gently on the stove with a splash of water or broth to loosen it up.

**How do I store leftovers?**

Pour leftover polenta into a greased container and refrigerate. It will firm up. You can slice and pan-fry or grill it for a different preparation.

**My polenta is too thick. What do I do?**

Whisk in hot water a few tablespoons at a time until you reach your desired consistency.
