## Ingredients for Maple-Miso Pie

**Pretzel Crust:**

- 170 g pretzels
- 112 g melted butter
- 67 g granulated sugar
- 2 tbsp white sesame seeds (about 20 g)

**Maple-Miso Custard:**

- 600 ml heavy cream (2 1/2 cups)
- 144 g brown sugar
- 50 g cornstarch
- 3 g kosher salt (1/2 tsp)
- 80 g egg yolks (about 4 large eggs)
- 120 ml maple syrup (1/2 cup)
- 60 g white miso (1/4 cup)
- 5 ml vanilla extract (1 tsp)

**Ingredient Notes:**

White miso is milder and sweeter than red or yellow miso. You can find it at most grocery stores in Montreal now, or at any Asian market. Look for it in the refrigerated section.

For pretzels, use the regular twisted kind, not pretzel rods or sticks. The traditional ones have the right texture when crushed.

Maple syrup should be real maple syrup, not pancake syrup. This is Montreal, we have standards.

**Substitutions:**

- No white miso? Yellow miso works but will be slightly more intense
- No sesame seeds? Leave them out or use crushed almonds
- No heavy cream? Use half-and-half but the custard will be less rich

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Make the Pretzel Crust**

Preheat your oven to 350°F (175°C). In a food processor, pulse the pretzels until they're a fine powder, like coarse sand. Add the melted butter, sugar, and sesame seeds. Pulse until everything is combined and looks like wet sand.

**Step 2: Form and Bake the Crust**

Press the pretzel mixture into a 9-inch pie tin, making sure to press it up the sides evenly. Freeze for 15 minutes (this helps it hold its shape), then bake for 10 minutes until lightly golden. Put it back in the freezer while you make the custard.

**Step 3: Start the Custard**

In a saucepan over medium heat, whisk together the cream, brown sugar, cornstarch, and salt. Keep whisking as it heats up. The mixture will start thin but will thicken as the cornstarch activates. This takes about 8-10 minutes. Here's the science: cornstarch granules absorb liquid when heated and swell up, creating that thick, silky texture.

**Step 4: Temper the Egg Yolks**

Put the egg yolks in a mixing bowl. When your cream mixture is thick, slowly pour about half of it into the egg yolks while whisking constantly. This is called tempering, and it prevents the eggs from scrambling. Once the yolks are warmed up, pour everything back into the saucepan.

**Step 5: Finish the Custard**

Over low heat, whisk in the maple syrup, white miso, and vanilla extract. Keep whisking until the miso is completely dissolved and the mixture is smooth. Remove from heat.

**Step 6: Assemble and Bake**

Pour the custard into your frozen pretzel crust. Bake at 350°F for 15 minutes until the edges are set but the center still has a slight jiggle. Let it cool to room temperature, then refrigerate for at least 2 hours before serving.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Maple-Miso Pie

**Don't Skip Tempering the Eggs**

If you pour hot cream directly into egg yolks, you'll get scrambled eggs in your custard. Temper slowly and whisk constantly.

**Freeze the Crust Between Steps**

Freezing helps the crust hold its shape during baking. Don't skip this or you'll end up with a sliding crust.

**The Center Should Jiggle Slightly**

When you take the pie out of the oven, the center should still wobble a bit when you shake the pan. It will set as it cools. Overbaking makes it grainy.

**Dissolve the Miso Completely**

Miso can be lumpy. Whisk it thoroughly into the warm custard so there are no chunks.

## FAQs About Maple-Miso Pie

**Can I taste the miso in the pie?**

You don't taste it as "miso" exactly. It adds a subtle savory depth and complexity that makes the pie taste more interesting than regular custard pie.

**How long does this pie last?**

It keeps in the fridge for up to 3 days covered with plastic wrap. The crust may soften slightly over time.

**Can I make this ahead?**

Yes, this is perfect to make the day before. In fact, the flavors develop and improve overnight.

**What if I can't find white miso?**

Yellow miso works but use slightly less (3 tbsp instead of ¼ cup) because it's stronger. Red miso is too intense for this recipe.

**Why is my custard grainy?**

Either the eggs scrambled from too much heat, or you overbaked it. Make sure to temper the eggs properly and take the pie out when the center still jiggles.
