## What Is Sous Vide

Sous vide (French for "under vacuum") cooks food in a precisely controlled water bath. You seal ingredients in a bag, submerge them in water held at the exact target temperature, and wait. The food reaches that temperature throughout and stays there without overcooking.

## Essential Equipment

### What You Need

- **Immersion circulator:** $100-200 (Anova, Joule are popular brands)
- **Container:** Any large pot or dedicated sous vide container
- **Bags:** Ziplock freezer bags work, vacuum sealer is optional
- **Cast iron pan or torch:** For finishing sear

### What's Nice to Have

- Vacuum sealer for better seal and longer storage
- Insulated container to reduce energy use
- Rack to keep bags separated

## Key Temperatures for Date Night Proteins

### Steak

- Rare: 120-125°F (1-4 hours)
- Medium-rare: 129-134°F (1-4 hours)
- Medium: 135-144°F (1-4 hours)

### Chicken Breast

- Tender and juicy: 140-145°F (1.5-4 hours)
- Traditional texture: 150°F (1-4 hours)

### Fish

- Salmon (medium): 125°F (30-45 minutes)
- Firm fish: 130°F (30-45 minutes)

### Eggs

- Soft poached style: 145°F (45 minutes)
- Custard-like: 167°F (13 minutes)

## The Basic Process

1. **Season the protein:** Salt, pepper, and any aromatics (garlic, herbs). Less is more with sous vide since flavors concentrate.
2. **Bag it:** Place in ziplock bag. Use water displacement method: slowly lower bag into water, letting pressure push out air before sealing.
3. **Set temperature:** Fill container with water. Set circulator to target temperature. Wait for it to reach temp (10-15 minutes).
4. **Cook:** Submerge sealed bag. Set timer. Walk away.
5. **Sear and serve:** Pat protein dry. Sear in screaming hot cast iron or with torch (1 minute per side). Serve immediately.

---

## Why It Works

**Precision:** Traditional cooking applies high heat from outside, creating gradients (overcooked edges, raw center). Sous vide heats evenly throughout to exactly your target temperature.

**Impossible to overcook:** Once food reaches target temp, it stays there. A steak at 130°F for 2 hours or 4 hours ends up the same (within reason).

**Timing flexibility:** This is crucial for date night. Start cooking early, finish when ready.

---

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake:** Skipping the sear

**Solution:** Always finish with high heat. The sear creates flavor and texture contrast.

**Mistake:** Overseasoning

**Solution:** Flavors concentrate in the bag. Use less salt and seasoning than normal.

**Mistake:** Wrong bag seal

**Solution:** Ensure all air is out. Floating bags cook unevenly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is sous vide safe?**

Yes. Extended cooking at lower temperatures still pasteurizes food. Follow time/temperature guidelines.

**Q: Can I cook frozen?**

Yes. Add 30-60 minutes to cooking time.

**Q: Do I need a vacuum sealer?**

No. Ziplock bags with water displacement work perfectly.

**Q: What if I don't have a torch?**

Cast iron pan works great. Get it screaming hot, sear 30-60 seconds per side.
