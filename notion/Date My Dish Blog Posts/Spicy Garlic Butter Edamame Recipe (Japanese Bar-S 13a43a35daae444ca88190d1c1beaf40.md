## Why You'll Love This Spicy Edamame

This isn't your standard steamed edamame with salt. This is Japanese izakaya-style edamame, tossed in garlic butter and spiked with togarashi. The edamame are blanched just until tender, then hit with butter in a hot pan. The butter browns slightly and the garlic gets fragrant and golden. A shake of togarashi adds heat and complexity. The soy sauce brings umami. What you end up with is edamame that's addictive. The pods are slick with butter, aromatic with garlic, and have this subtle heat that builds as you eat more. It's the kind of appetizer that disappears before the main course arrives. The best part is how fast it comes together. From frozen edamame to finished dish in about 10 minutes. It's perfect for last-minute dinner parties, date nights, or just when you want something salty and satisfying to snack on. The interactive nature of eating edamame, popping the beans out of the pods with your teeth, makes it a great social food. It keeps your hands busy and gives you something to do while you're talking.

## Ingredients for Spicy Garlic Butter Edamame

- 454 g frozen edamame in pods (1 lb / 16 oz)
- 1/4 cup soy sauce (60 ml)
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- Pinch of togarashi (Japanese seven-spice blend)
- 1 tbsp sesame oil
- 40 g butter (3 tbsp)

**Ingredient Notes:**

Frozen edamame in pods work perfectly. You can find them at any Asian grocery in Montreal or even at IGA and Metro in the frozen section.

Togarashi (shichimi togarashi) is a Japanese seven-spice blend with chili, sesame, nori, and citrus. You can find it at Miyamoto on Rue Sainte-Catherine or any Asian market. If you can't find it, substitute with red pepper flakes and a pinch of sesame seeds.

Use good quality soy sauce. Japanese soy sauce (shoyu) is less salty and more balanced than Chinese soy sauce.

The butter is what makes this recipe special. It creates a rich, glossy coating that clings to the pods.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Boil the Edamame**

Bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil. Add the frozen edamame directly from the freezer. The water temperature will drop when you add them. Wait for the water to come back to a full boil, then immediately drain the edamame. This usually takes 3-4 minutes total. Don't overcook them or they'll get mushy.

**Step 2: Drain Well**

Pour the edamame into a colander and shake well to remove excess water. You want them as dry as possible so the butter sauce will stick.

**Step 3: Heat the Pan**

Heat a large skillet or wok over medium-high heat. Let it get hot for about 1 minute.

**Step 4: Add Butter and Edamame**

Add the butter to the hot pan, then immediately add the drained edamame. The butter will start to melt and sizzle. Toss the edamame to coat them in the melting butter.

**Step 5: Add Garlic**

After 1 minute, add the minced garlic. Toss and sauté for another minute. The garlic should become fragrant and just start to turn golden. Don't let it burn or it will taste bitter.

**Step 6: Season**

Add a pinch of togarashi and the soy sauce. Toss everything together for about 30 seconds. The soy sauce will sizzle and reduce slightly, creating a glaze.

**Step 7: Finish with Sesame Oil**

Remove from heat and drizzle the sesame oil over the edamame. Toss one final time. The sesame oil adds a toasted, nutty aroma.

**Step 8: Serve**

Transfer to a serving bowl immediately. Serve hot with an empty bowl on the side for the discarded pods.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Spicy Edamame

**Don't Overcook the Edamame**

You want them tender but with a slight bite. Once the water comes back to a boil after adding the edamame, drain immediately.

**Dry the Edamame Well**

Excess water will dilute the butter sauce. Shake that colander hard.

**Work Fast in the Pan**

This recipe comes together in about 2 minutes of actual cooking time. Have everything prepped and ready to go.

**Watch the Garlic**

Garlic goes from perfect to burned in seconds. Keep tossing and don't walk away.

**Adjust the Heat**

Start with a small pinch of togarashi and add more to taste. You can always add more heat at the table.

## FAQs About Spicy Edamame

**Can I use fresh edamame instead of frozen?**

Yes, but fresh edamame will need to boil for 5-6 minutes instead of 3-4. Test one to make sure it's tender.

**What if I can't find togarashi?**

Mix red pepper flakes with sesame seeds and a tiny bit of orange zest. It won't be exactly the same but it'll be close.

**Can I make this ahead?**

Edamame is best served hot right after cooking. You can blanch the edamame ahead of time, but do the butter and garlic step just before serving.

**Is the sesame oil necessary?**

It adds a lot of flavor, but if you don't have it, you can skip it or add a sprinkle of toasted sesame seeds instead.

**How do you eat edamame?**

Hold the pod, put it in your mouth, and use your teeth to squeeze the beans out. Discard the pod. Don't eat the pod itself.

**Can I make this spicier?**

Absolutely. Add more togarashi, or throw in some red pepper flakes or fresh sliced chili peppers when you add the garlic.
