## Ingredients for Street Style Tteokbokki

**For the Sauce:**

- ⅔ cup (160 ml) sesame oil
- ⅔ cup (160 ml) soy sauce
- ¾ cup (170 g) cane sugar
- ¾ cup (85 g) gochugaru (Korean red pepper flakes)
- 6 tbsp (120 g) gochujang (Korean chili paste)
- 25 garlic cloves, minced
- 6 scallions, thinly sliced
- Pinch of finely ground black pepper
- ¾ cup (180 ml) water

**For the Rice Cakes:**

- 42 oz (1200 g) tteokbokki-style stick-shaped rice cakes
- 6 large eggs, at room temperature
- ¼ cup (60 ml) vegetable oil
- Pinch of granulated sugar
- 4 scallions, thinly sliced
- Pinch of chili threads (optional)

**Ingredient Notes:**

Tteokbokki rice cakes are cylinder-shaped Korean rice cakes. You can find them fresh or frozen at Asian grocery stores. If frozen, thaw them before soaking.

Gochugaru is Korean red pepper flakes. It's milder and slightly sweet compared to regular chili flakes. Essential for authentic flavor.

Gochujang is fermented Korean chili paste. Adds umami depth and heat. Available at most grocery stores now or any Asian market.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Make the Sauce**

In a large mixing bowl, combine the sesame oil, soy sauce, cane sugar, gochugaru, gochujang, minced garlic, sliced scallions, black pepper, and ¾ cup water. Whisk until the sugar has completely dissolved. The sauce should be thick and glossy. Cover and refrigerate until ready to use. This can be made up to 3 days ahead.

**Step 2: Prepare the Rice Cakes**

Make sure the rice cakes are not stuck together. Place them in a large bowl and cover with warm water. Soak for 15 minutes to soften them. This makes them more tender and helps them cook evenly. Drain well and set aside.

**Step 3: Cook the Eggs**

Bring a medium saucepan of water to a boil over high heat. Gently add the eggs and cook for 6 to 7 minutes for soft-boiled eggs with jammy yolks. Using a slotted spoon, transfer the eggs to a bowl of ice water immediately. Leave them in the ice water for at least 10 minutes until cool enough to handle, then peel carefully.

**Step 4: Cook the First Batch**

Heat half of the vegetable oil in a large nonstick skillet over high heat until shimmering. Reduce the heat to medium-high.

**Step 5: Add Rice Cakes and Sauce**

Add half of the drained rice cakes to the skillet and toss to coat in the oil. Spread them into an even layer. Let them sit for 1 to 2 minutes without stirring until they're lightly toasted on the bottom. This creates texture. Toss again. Once the rice cakes are crispy on the edges, add half of the sauce. Toss everything together to coat the rice cakes completely in the sauce. Cook for another 2-3 minutes until the sauce thickens and clings to the rice cakes.

**Step 6: Repeat with Second Batch**

Transfer the first batch to a plate and keep warm. Repeat the entire process with the remaining oil, rice cakes, and sauce.

**Step 7: Serve**

Divide the tteokbokki among 6 plates. Halve the soft-boiled eggs lengthwise and place on top of each serving. Garnish with the thinly sliced scallions and a pinch of chili threads if using. Serve immediately while hot.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Street Style Tteokbokki

**Don't Skip Soaking the Rice Cakes**

Soaking softens them and prevents them from being too chewy. If you skip this step, they won't absorb the sauce properly.

**Toast the Rice Cakes Before Adding Sauce**

Letting them sit undisturbed creates crispy edges that contrast beautifully with the sauce. Don't rush this step.

**Work in Batches**

Cooking in two batches ensures the pan stays hot enough to get good caramelization. Overcrowding the pan steams everything instead of crisping it.

**Adjust the Heat Level**

If you're sensitive to spice, reduce the gochugaru to ½ cup. The gochujang is essential for flavor, so don't reduce that too much.

## FAQs About Street Style Tteokbokki

**Can I add protein?**

You can add crispy tofu, mushrooms, or fish cakes. Pan-fry them until golden before adding the rice cakes.

**How do I store leftover sauce?**

The sauce keeps in the fridge for up to 2 weeks in an airtight container. It's also great on other dishes like stir-fries or as a marinade.

**Can I use a different type of rice cake?**

Stick to tteokbokki-style cylinder rice cakes. Other shapes won't have the same texture or cook the same way.

**Why are my rice cakes mushy?**

You either soaked them too long or cooked them too long in the sauce. Soak for exactly 15 minutes and cook just until they're coated and heated through.

**Can I make this less spicy?**

Reduce the gochugaru to ½ cup and use less sauce per batch. You can always add more at the table.
