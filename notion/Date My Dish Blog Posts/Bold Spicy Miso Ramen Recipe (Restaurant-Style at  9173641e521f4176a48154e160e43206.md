## Ingredients for Spicy Miso Ramen

**Spicy Miso Paste:**

- 130 g white miso paste (1/2 cup)
- 130 g red miso paste (1/2 cup)
- 80 g Sichuan douban chili paste (1/3 cup)
- 1 small onion, roughly chopped
- 6 garlic cloves, crushed
- 30 g fresh ginger, roughly chopped
- 45 ml mirin (3 tbsp)
- 30 ml vegetable oil (2 tbsp)
- 15 ml toasted sesame oil (1 tbsp)
- 8 g dashi granules (1 tbsp)
- 15 g sesame paste (2 tsp)

**Garlic Togarashi Oil:**

- 2 small shallots, finely minced
- 2 garlic cloves, finely minced
- 1/2 tsp sesame seeds (2 g)
- Pinch of sea salt
- 60 ml vegetable oil (1/4 cup)
- 2 1/2 tbsp shichimi togarashi (Japanese seven spice)

**For the Ramen:**

- 220 g ground pork
- 15 ml toasted sesame oil (1 tbsp)
- 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper (1 g)
- 475 g unsalted chicken or pork broth (2 cups)
- 227 g unsweetened unflavored soy milk (1 cup)
- 180 g spicy miso paste (3/4 cup total)
- 2 portions fresh ramen noodles
- 60 g green onions, finely diced (4 tbsp)
- 1 sheet nori seaweed, cut into rectangles
- 75 g fresh corn (1/2 cup)

**Ingredient Notes:**

Douban paste is fermented chili bean paste from Sichuan. It's spicy and deeply savory. You can find it at Asian grocery stores in Montreal.

Shichimi togarashi is Japanese seven spice blend. It has chili, sesame, orange peel, and other aromatics. Essential for that smoky heat.

Fresh ramen noodles make a huge difference. Look for them in the refrigerated section of Asian markets, not the dried instant kind.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Make the Spicy Miso Paste**

In a food processor, combine both misos, douban paste, onion, garlic, ginger, mirin, both oils, dashi granules, and sesame paste. Blend until smooth. This makes more than you need for two bowls, but it keeps for weeks in the fridge. Store the extra in an airtight container.

**Step 2: Make the Garlic Togarashi Oil**

In a small pan, combine shallots, garlic, sesame seeds, salt, and vegetable oil. Heat over medium-low heat until everything is fragrant and the shallots are golden, about 5 minutes. Remove from heat and stir in the togarashi. Set aside.

**Step 3: Cook the Pork**

In a large pot over medium-high heat, cook the ground pork with sesame oil and black pepper, breaking it up as it cooks, until browned and crispy, about 5-7 minutes.

**Step 4: Build the Broth**

Add the chicken broth and soy milk to the pot with the pork. Bring to a simmer. Whisk in 3/4 cup of the spicy miso paste until completely dissolved. Taste and adjust. The broth should be rich, creamy, and spicy.

**Step 5: Cook the Noodles**

In a separate pot, cook the fresh ramen noodles according to package directions, usually 2-3 minutes. Drain immediately.

**Step 6: Assemble the Bowls**

Divide the noodles between two large bowls. Ladle the hot broth and pork over the noodles. Add ramen eggs if desired, then top with green onions, nori sheets, and corn. Drizzle with the garlic togarashi oil.

**Step 7: Serve**

Serve immediately while everything is hot. Encourage mixing everything together before eating.

## FAQs About Spicy Miso Ramen

**Can I make this vegetarian?**

Yes. Skip the pork, use vegetable broth, and replace the dashi granules with kombu powder. The eggs are optional.

**How spicy is this?**

Pretty spicy from the douban paste and togarashi. If you're sensitive to heat, start with half the amount of spicy miso paste and add more to taste.

**Can I freeze the miso paste?**

Yes, it freezes well for up to 3 months. Thaw in the fridge overnight before using.

**What if I can't find fresh ramen noodles?**

Look for dried ramen noodles (not instant) at Asian markets. They're better than instant but not as good as fresh.

**What toppings work best?**

Beyond the classic corn, nori, and green onions, try adding ramen eggs, bamboo shoots, or bean sprouts.
