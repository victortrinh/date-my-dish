## Choosing Your Steak

### Best Cuts for Date Night

**Ribeye**

- Marbling: Excellent (most flavorful)
- Tenderness: Very tender
- Thickness: 1.5 inches ideal
- Why: Forgiving due to high fat content, hard to overcook

**Strip Steak (New York Strip)**

- Marbling: Good
- Tenderness: Tender with slight chew
- Thickness: 1-1.5 inches
- Why: Balance of flavor and texture, less fatty than ribeye

**Filet Mignon**

- Marbling: Minimal
- Tenderness: Most tender cut
- Thickness: 1.5-2 inches
- Why: Melt-in-mouth texture, elegant presentation

## Quality Indicators

**Look for:**

✅ Bright red color (not gray or brown)

✅ Visible marbling (white fat throughout)

✅ Thick cut (minimum 1 inch, preferably 1.5 inches)

✅ Dry surface (not sitting in liquid)

✅ Grade: USDA Prime or Choice

**Avoid:**

❌ Thin steaks (under 1 inch)

❌ Gray or brown discoloration

❌ Excessive liquid in package

❌ Strong smell

## Essential Preparation Steps

### Step 1: Bring to Room Temperature

**Time:** 30-60 minutes before cooking

**Why:** Cold steak cooks unevenly. Room temperature steak develops better crust and cooks more uniformly.

**How:** Remove from fridge, leave on counter loosely covered.

---

### Step 2: Pat Completely Dry

**Why:** Surface moisture creates steam, prevents proper searing.

**How:** Use paper towels to thoroughly dry both sides and edges.

---

### Step 3: Season Generously

**When:** Just before cooking (or 40 minutes ahead for dry-brining)

**What:** Kosher salt and coarsely ground black pepper

**How much:** More than you think. About 1/2 teaspoon salt per side for a 1-pound steak.

## The Pan-Sear Method (Most Reliable)

### Equipment Needed

- Heavy cast iron or stainless steel pan
- High smoke point oil (avocado, vegetable)
- Instant-read thermometer
- Butter, garlic, and fresh herbs (optional finishing)

### Steps

**Step 1: Preheat pan (5 minutes)**

Heat pan over high heat until smoking. This is crucial. The pan must be very hot.

**Step 2: Add oil**

Add 1-2 tablespoons high smoke point oil. Swirl to coat.

**Step 3: Add steak**

Lay steak away from you to avoid splatter. Don't move it.

**Step 4: Sear first side (3-4 minutes)**

Leave it alone. Resist the urge to peek. A crust is forming.

**Step 5: Flip once**

Use tongs. Steak should release easily when ready.

**Step 6: Sear second side (3-4 minutes)**

Add butter, garlic, and thyme if desired. Baste with melted butter.

**Step 7: Check temperature**

Insert thermometer into thickest part:

- Rare: 120-125°F
- Medium-rare: 130-135°F
- Medium: 135-145°F

**Step 8: Rest (5-10 minutes)**

Move to cutting board or plate. Tent loosely with foil. Temperature will rise 5°F during rest.

## The Reverse Sear Method (Most Control)

Best for thick steaks (1.5 inches or more) when you want edge-to-edge even cooking.

**Step 1: Preheat oven to 275°F**

**Step 2: Season steak, place on wire rack over baking sheet**

**Step 3: Roast until 10-15°F below target temp (20-40 minutes)**

Use thermometer to monitor.

**Step 4: Remove and rest (10 minutes)**

**Step 5: Sear in screaming hot pan (1 minute per side)**

Quick sear for crust without overcooking interior.

**Result:** Perfect doneness throughout with excellent crust.

## Temperature Guide (Internal Temp)

### Rare (120-125°F)

- Cool red center
- Very soft texture
- Maximum juiciness

### Medium-Rare (130-135°F) - Recommended

- Warm red center
- Tender, juicy
- Ideal for most cuts

### Medium (135-145°F)

- Warm pink center
- Slightly firm
- Still juicy

### Medium-Well (145-155°F)

- Mostly gray with hint of pink
- Firm texture
- Drier

### Well-Done (160°F+)

- Gray throughout
- Very firm
- Significantly drier

**Pro tip:** Always pull steak 5°F below target. It continues cooking while resting.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake:** Not preheating pan enough

**Solution:** Pan should smoke before steak goes in. This takes 5 full minutes.

**Mistake:** Moving the steak too much

**Solution:** Let it sit undisturbed to develop crust. Flip only once.

**Mistake:** Cutting into it immediately

**Solution:** Rest 5-10 minutes. Juices redistribute, steak stays moist.

**Mistake:** Using lean cuts for high heat

**Solution:** Filet mignon needs gentler cooking or butter basting. Ribeye can handle high heat.

**Mistake:** Guessing doneness

**Solution:** Use a thermometer. It's the only reliable method.

## The Basting Technique

Adds rich flavor and helps develop crust.

**When:** Last 2 minutes of cooking

**What:** 2 tablespoons butter, 2 garlic cloves (smashed), 2 sprigs fresh thyme

**How:** Tilt pan, use spoon to pour melted butter over steak repeatedly (30-40 seconds).

**Result:** Enhanced flavor, golden crust, aromatic presentation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do I know when the pan is hot enough?**

Hold your hand 6 inches above the pan. If you can't keep it there for more than 2 seconds, it's ready. Or add a drop of water—it should evaporate instantly.

**Q: Should I oil the steak or the pan?**

Either works. Oiling the pan is easier and prevents oversaturating the steak.

**Q: My steak always sticks to the pan. Why?**

Pan isn't hot enough, or you're moving the steak too soon. When a proper crust forms, it releases naturally.

**Q: Can I cook steak from frozen?**

Yes, but it requires the reverse sear method. Add 50% more time to the oven portion.

**Q: What if I don't have a thermometer?**

The hand test (touching your palm and comparing firmness) works but is less reliable. Invest in an instant-read thermometer.
