## What Is Velveting

Velveting is a Chinese cooking technique that coats protein in a mixture of cornstarch, egg white, and sometimes oil or wine. This creates a protective barrier that keeps meat tender during high-heat cooking. The result is that silky, tender texture you get at good Chinese restaurants.

## The Basic Velveting Technique

### Ingredients Per Pound of Protein

- 1 egg white
- 1 tablespoon cornstarch
- 1 tablespoon rice wine or water
- 1/2 teaspoon salt
- Optional: 1 tablespoon oil

### Steps

1. **Slice the protein thin:** Cut chicken, beef, or pork into thin, uniform pieces (about 1/4 inch). Uniform size ensures even cooking.
2. **Mix the coating:** Whisk egg white until slightly frothy. Add cornstarch, wine, and salt. Mix until smooth.
3. **Coat the protein:** Toss protein pieces in the mixture until evenly coated. Let sit for 15-30 minutes at room temperature.
4. **Pre-cook (optional but recommended):** Briefly blanch in oil (30 seconds at 280°F) or boiling water (1-2 minutes). This sets the coating.
5. **Stir-fry as normal:** Add to your hot wok with vegetables and sauce. The meat stays tender throughout.

## Why Velveting Works

**Science:** The cornstarch and egg white create a physical barrier that prevents moisture loss. When proteins cook, their fibers contract and squeeze out water. This coating keeps that moisture locked in, resulting in tender meat even at high temperatures.

**Temperature control:** The pre-cooking step gently sets the coating before the aggressive heat of stir-frying. This two-stage cooking prevents the meat from seizing up.

---

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

**Mistake:** Skipping the rest time

**Solution:** Let the coating adhere for at least 15 minutes. This ensures it stays on during cooking.

**Mistake:** Cutting meat too thick

**Solution:** Thin, uniform pieces (1/4 inch) cook quickly and stay tender.

**Mistake:** Overcrowding the wok

**Solution:** Cook in batches if needed. Overcrowding drops the temperature and steams instead of sears.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I velvet in advance?**

Yes. Coat and refrigerate for up to 4 hours before cooking. Bring to room temperature before stir-frying.

**Is the pre-cooking step necessary?**

Not required, but it gives the best results. For quick weeknight cooking, you can skip it and still get tender meat.

**What if I don't have rice wine?**

Water, dry sherry, or even vodka work. The alcohol helps tenderize and adds minimal flavor.

**Can I use whole eggs instead of just whites?**

Yes, but egg whites give a cleaner, more delicate coating. Whole eggs work in a pinch.
