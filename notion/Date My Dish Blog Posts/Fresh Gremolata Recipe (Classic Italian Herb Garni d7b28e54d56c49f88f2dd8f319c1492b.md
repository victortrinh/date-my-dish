## Ingredients for Gremolata

- 4 tbsp fresh parsley, minced (about 1/4 cup)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tsp lemon zest, freshly grated
- 2 cloves garlic, crushed or minced
- Pinch of salt and black pepper to taste

**Ingredient Notes:**

Flat-leaf Italian parsley is traditional and has more flavor than curly parsley. You can find it at any grocery store in Montreal.

Use a microplane or fine grater for the lemon zest. You want just the yellow part, not the white pith which is bitter.

Crush the garlic with the side of your knife before mincing it, or use a garlic press. Fresh garlic is essential.

Good quality olive oil matters here since it's one of only five ingredients. Use extra virgin olive oil.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Prep the Parsley**

Wash and thoroughly dry the fresh parsley. Remove the leaves from the stems and discard the stems. Mince the parsley as finely as you can. You want it almost paste-like. The finer you chop it, the better it will mix with the other ingredients.

**Step 2: Zest the Lemon**

Using a microplane or fine grater, zest the lemon. Make sure you're only getting the yellow part, not the white pith underneath. You need about 2 tsp of zest, which is usually one medium lemon.

**Step 3: Crush and Mince the Garlic**

Peel the garlic cloves. Place the side of your knife over each clove and press down firmly to crush it. Then mince it finely. Alternatively, use a garlic press.

**Step 4: Mix Everything**

In a small bowl, combine the minced parsley, olive oil, lemon zest, and crushed garlic. Add a pinch of salt and black pepper. Stir well until everything is evenly mixed. The mixture should be moist but not soupy.

**Step 5: Taste and Adjust**

Taste the gremolata. If it needs more brightness, add a bit more lemon zest. If it needs more punch, add more garlic. Adjust salt and pepper to taste.

**Step 6: Serve**

Use immediately for the best flavor, or cover and refrigerate for up to 2 hours. Gremolata loses its brightness if made too far ahead.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Gremolata

**Use Fresh Ingredients**

This cannot be stressed enough. Dried parsley, bottled lemon juice, or jarred garlic will not give you the same result.

**Mince the Parsley Finely**

The finer you chop the parsley, the better it incorporates with the other ingredients.

**Zest, Don't Juice**

You want lemon zest, not lemon juice. The zest has all the aromatic oils without the acidity.

**Make It Fresh**

Gremolata is best made within a few hours of serving. The garlic gets stronger and the parsley loses its brightness over time.

**Adjust to Taste**

Some people like more garlic, some like more lemon. Taste and adjust before serving.

## FAQs About Gremolata

**Can I make gremolata ahead?**

You can make it a few hours ahead and refrigerate it, but it's best made fresh. The longer it sits, the more the garlic intensifies and the parsley loses its brightness.

**Can I use dried parsley?**

No. Fresh parsley is essential. Dried parsley doesn't have the flavor or color you need.

**What if I don't have a microplane for the lemon zest?**

Use the finest side of a box grater. Just be careful to only get the yellow part, not the white pith.

**Can I add other herbs?**

Yes. Some versions include rosemary or thyme. Just make sure parsley is still the dominant herb.

**How do I store leftovers?**

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 2 days. The flavor will change as it sits, becoming more garlicky and less bright.

**Is gremolata the same as chimichurri?**

No. While both are herb-based condiments, chimichurri has vinegar or lemon juice, more oil, and often includes cilantro and oregano. Gremolata is simpler and doesn't have acid from juice.
