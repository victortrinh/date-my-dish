## Ingredients for Ramen Eggs

**For the Marinade:**

- 50 ml sake (3 1/2 tbsp)
- 50 ml mirin (3 1/2 tbsp)
- 200 ml soy sauce (1 3/4 cup + 1 tbsp)
- 30 g sugar (2 tbsp)
- 40 g garlic, coarsely chopped (3 tbsp)
- 75 g fresh ginger, coarsely chopped (2 1/2 oz)
- 1 L water (1 quart)

**For the Eggs:**

- 6 large fresh eggs, room temperature
- Water for boiling
- Ice for ice bath

**Ingredient Notes:**

Room temperature eggs are essential. Cold eggs from the fridge will crack when they hit boiling water. Let them sit out for 30 minutes before cooking.

The marinade makes more than you need for 6 eggs, but it keeps for up to a week in the fridge and can be reused once.

Fresh eggs are harder to peel than older eggs. If you can, buy your eggs 3-5 days before making this recipe.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Make the Marinade**

In a saucepan over medium-high heat, simmer the sake and mirin for 2 minutes. This cooks off some of the alcohol. Reduce heat to low and add the soy sauce, sugar, garlic, and ginger. Simmer and stir for 10 minutes. Remove from heat and let cool completely to room temperature. You can make this up to a week ahead and store it in the fridge.

**Step 2: Prepare the Soaking Liquid**

In a large bowl, combine the cooled marinade with 1 L of water. This dilutes the marinade so it flavors the eggs without making them too salty. Set aside.

**Step 3: Bring Water to a Boil**

Fill a large pot with water and bring it to a rolling boil. Use a big pot. When you add the eggs, the temperature will drop, and you want it to come back to a boil quickly.

**Step 4: Pierce the Eggs**

Using a pushpin or thumbtack, poke a small hole in the bottom (the larger, rounder end) of each egg. This allows air to escape and prevents cracking. Here's the science: there's an air pocket at the wide end of the egg, and when it heats up, the air expands and can crack the shell. The hole releases that pressure.

**Step 5: Cook the Eggs**

Gently slide the eggs into the boiling water. Start your timer immediately. Stir gently for the first 2 minutes to help center the yolks. The total cooking time is exactly 6 minutes and 10 seconds for large eggs. Not 6 minutes, not 6:30. Exactly 6:10.

**Step 6: Ice Bath**

While the eggs cook, prepare a large bowl filled with ice water. When the timer hits 6:10, immediately remove the eggs with a slotted spoon and plunge them into the ice bath. Stir the water to make sure there are no hot pockets. This stops the cooking process instantly.

**Step 7: Peel the Eggs**

Once the eggs are completely cool, about 15 minutes, peel them. Start at the wide end where you poked the hole. The membrane should separate more easily there. Peel under running water if you're having trouble.

**Step 8: Marinate**

Place the peeled eggs in the marinade mixture. Make sure they're completely submerged. Refrigerate for at least 2 hours. They'll keep in the marinade for up to 3 days and get more flavorful over time.

**Step 9: Slice and Serve**

When ready to serve, remove an egg from the marinade and pat it dry. Use a sharp knife or fishing line to cut it in half. The yolk should be creamy and barely set, with that perfect jammy consistency.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Ramen Eggs

**The 6:10 Rule is Non-Negotiable**

That 10 extra seconds beyond 6 minutes is what gives you the perfect texture. Set a timer. Don't guess.

**Room Temperature Eggs Matter**

Cold eggs will crack in boiling water, and the timing will be off. Let them sit out for 30 minutes.

**Don't Skip the Pushpin Hole**

This simple step prevents 90% of cracking issues. Pierce the wide end where the air pocket is.

**Ice Bath Immediately**

Don't let the eggs sit for even 30 seconds after cooking. The residual heat will continue cooking them and you'll lose that jammy yolk.

**Use Fresh Eggs for Eating, Older Eggs for Peeling**

Fresh eggs taste better but are harder to peel. Eggs that are 5-7 days old peel much easier. Choose based on your priorities.

## FAQs About Ramen Eggs

**Why 6 minutes and 10 seconds specifically?**

This timing gives you fully set whites with a yolk that's creamy and barely set. It's the perfect jammy texture. Adjust by 10-20 seconds if your eggs are extra large or medium.

**Can I make these ahead?**

Yes. They keep in the marinade for up to 3 days and actually get better as they marinate longer.

**What if my eggs crack while cooking?**

It happens. The egg white will leak out a bit, but they're usually still usable. The pushpin hole and room temperature eggs prevent most cracking.

**Can I reuse the marinade?**

You can use it one more time for a second batch of eggs, but the flavor will be weaker. Don't use it more than twice.

**How do I slice them without the yolk spilling out?**

Use a very sharp knife and wipe it clean between cuts. Or use taut fishing line, which cuts cleanly without putting pressure on the yolk.

**My yolks are too hard/soft. What went wrong?**

Too hard: You cooked them too long or didn't ice bath immediately. Too soft: You didn't cook them long enough, or your eggs were smaller than large. Adjust timing by 10-20 seconds.
