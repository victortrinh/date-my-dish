## What Is Wok Hei

Wok hei (Cantonese for "breath of the wok") is that smoky, slightly charred flavor in good stir-fries. It comes from cooking at extremely high heat in a seasoned carbon steel wok. The flavor is a combination of seared proteins, caramelized sugars, and compounds created when oil hits the wok's super-hot surface.

## The Home Kitchen Challenge

Professional wok burners produce 50,000-100,000 BTUs. Most home gas stoves top out at 15,000 BTUs. Electric stoves are worse. This massive heat difference makes authentic wok hei nearly impossible at home using traditional methods.

But nearly impossible doesn't mean impossible.

## Techniques to Maximize Wok Hei

### Method 1: Work in Tiny Batches

**Why it works:** Less food means higher effective heat per ingredient.

**How to do it:**

- Cook no more than 1/2 cup of food at a time
- Keep everything moving constantly
- Remove to a plate, repeat in batches
- Combine at the end with sauce

**Downside:** Time-consuming, harder to coordinate

---

### Method 2: Use the Oven Boost

**Why it works:** Preheating the wok in the oven gets it hotter than your stovetop alone.

**How to do it:**

- Preheat carbon steel wok in 500°F oven for 10 minutes
- Transfer to highest stovetop burner
- Add oil (it should smoke immediately)
- Cook in small batches quickly

**Downside:** Requires carbon steel wok, oven mitt handling

---

### Method 3: Outdoor Wok Burner

**Why it works:** Portable propane burners reach 60,000+ BTUs.

**How to do it:**

- Set up outdoor wok burner
- Use on patio or outdoor space
- Cook as you would on professional equipment

**Downside:** Requires equipment purchase, outdoor space

## Essential Setup Tips

### Equipment

- **Carbon steel wok:** Round bottom, 14-inch diameter
- **Wok ring:** Stabilizes wok on burner (for round bottom woks)
- **Metal spatula:** For tossing and scraping
- **Mise en place:** Everything prepped before cooking starts

### Preparation Rules

- Cut all ingredients uniform size
- Pat proteins completely dry
- Have sauce mixed and ready
- Work within arm's reach of stove

### The Golden Rule

**Never stop moving the food.** Constant motion prevents burning while building char.

---

## The Cooking Sequence

1. **Heat wok until smoking:** 2-3 minutes
2. **Add oil in thin stream around sides:** It should smoke instantly.
3. **Add aromatics:** Garlic, ginger for 10 seconds
4. **Add protein:** Spread in single layer, let sit 30 seconds to sear, then toss.
5. **Add vegetables:** In order of cooking time. Firm vegetables first, leafy ones last.
6. **Add sauce:** Toss to coat, cook 30 seconds until thickened.
7. **Serve immediately:** Total cooking time: 3-4 minutes max.

## Common Mistakes

**Mistake:** Overcrowding the wok

**Solution:** Small batches maintain high heat. Food should sizzle loudly, not steam.

**Mistake:** Wet ingredients

**Solution:** Pat everything dry. Water drops temperature and prevents browning.

**Mistake:** Low heat

**Solution:** Maximum heat, always. You can't get wok hei without high heat.

**Mistake:** Slow cooking

**Solution:** Speed is essential. Ingredients should cook in minutes, not tens of minutes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I use a nonstick wok?**

No. Nonstick coatings can't handle the required heat. Carbon steel or cast iron only.

**Q: What oil should I use?**

High smoke point oils: peanut, vegetable, or canola. Avoid olive oil or butter.

**Q: Is the smoke normal?**

Yes. Good wok cooking produces smoke. Open windows and use your exhaust fan.

**Q: How do I season a new wok?**

Heat until blue-grey, add oil, spread with paper towel, repeat several times.
