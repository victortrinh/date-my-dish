## Ingredients for Karaage Chicken

**Main Ingredients:**

- 2.3 kg boneless, skinless chicken thighs (about 5 lbs)
- 5 g grated fresh ginger (1 tsp)
- 1 garlic clove, grated
- 45 ml sake (3 tbsp)
- 45 ml soy sauce (3 tbsp)
- 5 g salt (1 tsp)
- 5 g black pepper (1 tsp)
- 600 g potato starch (5 cups)
- 300 g all-purpose flour (2 1/2 cups)
- 1 egg
- Vegetable oil for frying

**For Serving:**

- Lemon wedges
- Shredded lettuce
- Japanese mayonnaise (Kewpie)
- Wasabi aioli (optional)

**Ingredient Notes:**

Chicken thighs are essential here. They stay juicier than breast meat and have more flavor. Cut them into bite-sized pieces, about 5cm chunks.

Potato starch is different from cornstarch. It creates a lighter, crispier coating. You can find it at Asian grocery stores in Montreal or Jean-Talon Market.

Sake is Japanese rice wine. If you can't find it, dry sherry works as a substitute, but sake is better.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Marinate the Chicken**

In a large bowl, combine the chicken pieces with grated ginger, garlic, sake, soy sauce, salt, and pepper. Mix well with your hands to coat everything evenly. Cover and refrigerate for 1 to 2 hours. Don't marinate longer than 2 hours or the chicken gets mushy from the sake breaking down the proteins too much.

**Step 2: Prepare the Potato Starch**

Put the potato starch in a bowl. Slowly drizzle in water, a little at a time, while stirring with chopsticks or a fork. You want the starch to form into clumps and chunks, not dissolve into a smooth paste. This creates texture in the coating. Set aside.

**Step 3: Add Flour and Egg to Chicken**

Take the chicken out of the fridge. Add the all-purpose flour and egg directly to the marinated chicken. Mix thoroughly with your hands until the chicken pieces are coated in a thick, sticky batter. This batter helps the potato starch stick.

**Step 4: Heat the Oil (First Temperature)**

Fill a heavy pot or deep fryer with oil to about 8cm deep. Heat to 325°F (163°C). Use a thermometer. Temperature control is crucial for this technique.

**Step 5: Coat and First Fry**

Take each piece of chicken and press it into the clumpy potato starch, coating it completely. The clumps create that extra-crunchy texture. Carefully lower the coated pieces into the 325°F oil. Don't overcrowd the pot. Fry for 90 seconds. This gentle fry cooks the chicken through without browning it too much. Remove and place on a wire rack.

**Step 6: Rest and Increase Temperature**

Let the chicken rest on the rack for 5 minutes while you increase the oil temperature to 350°F (177°C). This resting period is important. It lets the coating set.

**Step 7: Second Fry**

Once the oil reaches 350°F, fry the chicken pieces again for 45 seconds or until deep golden brown. Here's the science: the higher temperature creates the Maillard reaction, which gives you that golden color and crispy texture. The second fry dehydrates the coating, making it extra crunchy.

**Step 8: Drain and Serve**

Remove the chicken and let it drain on a wire rack or paper towels. Serve immediately with lemon wedges, shredded lettuce, and Japanese mayo.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Karaage

**Don't Skip the Double Fry**

The two-stage frying is what makes karaage different from regular fried chicken. The first fry cooks it, the second fry makes it crispy.

**Use a Thermometer**

Oil temperature matters. Too hot and the outside burns before the inside cooks. Too cool and it absorbs oil and gets greasy.

**Don't Overcrowd the Pan**

Frying too many pieces at once drops the oil temperature. Work in batches of 4-5 pieces.

**The Clumpy Potato Starch is Intentional**

Those clumps create extra crispy bits. Don't try to make it smooth.

## FAQs About Karaage Chicken

**Can I use chicken breast instead of thighs?**

You can, but thighs are juicier and more forgiving. If using breast, be extra careful not to overcook.

**What if I don't have potato starch?**

Cornstarch works in a pinch but won't be quite as light and crispy. The texture will be different.

**Can I skip the sake?**

Sake adds flavor and helps tenderize the chicken. Dry sherry is the best substitute. Avoid sweet cooking wines.

**Why do I need to fry twice?**

The first fry cooks the chicken gently. The second fry at higher heat creates the crispy golden exterior. It's the key to karaage's texture.

**How do I keep it crispy?**

Serve immediately for best crunch. If you need to hold it, keep it on a wire rack in a warm oven, not covered or it will steam and get soggy.
