## Ingredients for Chimichurri Sauce

- 1/2 cup olive oil (120 ml)
- 2 tbsp red wine vinegar
- 1/2 cup fresh parsley, finely chopped (about 1/4 cup packed)
- 3-4 cloves garlic, finely chopped or minced
- 2 small red chilies, deseeded and finely chopped (or 1 large, about 1 tbsp chopped)
- 3/4 tsp dried oregano
- 1 tsp coarse salt
- 1/2 tsp black pepper

**Ingredient Notes:**

Flat-leaf parsley works best. Curly parsley will work but has less flavor. You can find fresh parsley at any Montreal grocery store.

Red wine vinegar is traditional. Don't substitute with balsamic or white vinegar as they'll change the flavor profile significantly.

Fresh red chilies add the right amount of heat. If you can't find them, use 1/2 tsp of red pepper flakes instead.

Dried oregano is actually preferred over fresh here. The concentrated flavor works better in the sauce.

Use good quality extra virgin olive oil. It's half the recipe by volume, so quality matters.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Prep the Herbs and Aromatics**

Wash and thoroughly dry the fresh parsley. Remove the leaves from the stems and discard the stems. Chop the parsley as finely as you can. Finely mince the garlic cloves. If using fresh red chilies, cut them in half lengthwise, remove the seeds and white membrane, then chop them very finely.

**Step 2: Combine Everything**

In a medium bowl, combine the chopped parsley, minced garlic, chopped chilies, and dried oregano. Add the salt and black pepper. Pour in the olive oil and red wine vinegar. Stir everything together until well combined. The mixture should be loose and pourable, not thick like a paste.

**Step 3: Let It Rest**

Cover the bowl and let the chimichurri sit at room temperature for at least 10 minutes. This allows the flavors to start developing. For best results, refrigerate for 2 hours or up to 24 hours before using. The longer rest gives the garlic time to mellow and the herbs time to infuse the oil.

**Step 4: Taste and Adjust**

Before serving, taste the chimichurri and adjust seasoning if needed. Add more salt if it tastes flat, more vinegar if it needs brightness, or more chili if you want more heat.

**Step 5: Serve**

Bring the chimichurri to room temperature if it's been refrigerated. Stir well before serving as the oil and vinegar may separate. Spoon generously over grilled meats or use as a basting sauce while grilling.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Chimichurri

**Chop the Parsley Finely**

The finer you chop the parsley, the better it incorporates into the oil. You want small flecks, not big chunks.

**Let It Rest**

Don't skip the resting time. Fresh chimichurri tastes harsh and raw. After two hours, it becomes smooth and balanced.

**Adjust the Heat**

Start with one chili if you're heat-sensitive. You can always add more, but you can't take it away.

**Don't Blend It**

Chimichurri should be chopped by hand, not blended. Blending makes it too smooth and releases bitter flavors from the parsley stems.

**Use It Fresh**

Chimichurri is best within 24 hours. After that, the garlic intensifies and the parsley starts to brown.

## Variations

**Spicy Chimichurri**

Double the amount of red chilies or add 1/2 tsp cayenne pepper for extra heat.

**Cilantro Chimichurri**

Replace half the parsley with fresh cilantro for a different flavor profile.

**Lemon Chimichurri**

Add 1 tsp of lemon zest for brightness. Keep the red wine vinegar.

## FAQs About Chimichurri

**Can I make chimichurri ahead?**

Yes. Make it up to 24 hours ahead and refrigerate. The flavor actually improves after a few hours of resting.

**Can I freeze chimichurri?**

You can freeze it in ice cube trays for up to 3 months, but the texture changes and it won't be quite as vibrant when thawed.

**What's the difference between chimichurri and gremolata?**

Chimichurri has vinegar, oregano, and chilies, making it tangier and spicier. [Fresh Gremolata Recipe (Classic Italian Herb Garnish)](Fresh%20Gremolata%20Recipe%20(Classic%20Italian%20Herb%20Garni%20d7b28e54d56c49f88f2dd8f319c1492b.md) is more delicate with just parsley, lemon zest, and garlic.

**Can I use dried parsley?**

No. Fresh parsley is absolutely essential. Dried parsley has no flavor and won't give you the right result.

**Why is my chimichurri bitter?**

You either over-processed it in a blender or included too many parsley stems. Always chop by hand and use only the leaves.

**How long does chimichurri last?**

In the fridge, it keeps for up to 3 days, but it's best within 24 hours. The garlic gets stronger and the parsley loses its brightness over time.
