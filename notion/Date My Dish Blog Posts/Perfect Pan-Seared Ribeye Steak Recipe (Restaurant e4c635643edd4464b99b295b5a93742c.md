## Ingredients for Perfect Ribeye Steak

**For the Steaks:**

- 2 bone-in ribeye steaks, at least 1½ inches thick, about 1 pound (450 g) each
- Kosher salt
- 2 tbsp (30 ml) oil
- Freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp (30 g) butter
- A few thyme sprigs and sliced shallots (optional)

**Ingredient Notes:**

Bone-in ribeyes are essential. The bone adds flavor and helps with even cooking. At least 1½ inches thick.

Kosher salt for seasoning. Don't use table salt. The larger crystals give better coverage.

High smoke point oil like vegetable or canola. Not olive oil, it'll burn.

Butter for basting at the end. Adds richness and helps build that crust.

Thyme and shallots are optional but add aromatics. Don't skip the butter though.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Salt and Rest**

Pat steaks dry with paper towels. Season liberally with salt on all sides. Allow to rest at room temperature for at least 40 minutes and up to 2 hours. Alternatively, place on a plate or on a rack over a baking sheet in the refrigerator overnight, then remove from refrigerator at least 40 minutes before cooking. This brings the steak to room temperature and allows the salt to penetrate.

**Step 2: Heat the Pan**

In a large stainless-steel or cast iron skillet, heat oil over high heat until heavily smoking. The pan needs to be screaming hot for a proper sear.

**Step 3: Sear the Steaks**

Season steaks with pepper. Add to pan and cook, flipping frequently with tongs until well browned on all sides. Don't forget to sear the edges by holding steaks sideways with tongs. Cook until the internal temperature has reached 110°F (43°C) for rare or 130°F (54°C) for medium (steak will continue to cook afterward), 6 to 12 minutes depending on thickness.

**Step 4: Butter Baste**

Add butter and optional thyme and shallots to pan. Continue to cook, flipping often and spooning the melted butter over the steaks, for an additional 2 minutes. This builds flavor and adds a final layer of crust.

**Step 5: Rest**

Remove from pan and let rest in a warm place for at least 5 minutes before serving. The juices redistribute during the rest. Don't skip this.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Ribeye Steak

**Salt in Advance**

At least 40 minutes before cooking, or overnight in the fridge. This seasons the meat deeply, not just the surface.

**Room Temperature is Essential**

Cold steak cooks unevenly. Always bring to room temperature before cooking.

**Flip Frequently**

Contrary to old advice, flipping frequently gives more even cooking and better crust development.

**Use a Thermometer**

Don't guess. Use an instant-read thermometer. Pull at 110°F for rare, 130°F for medium.

**High Heat is Non-Negotiable**

The pan needs to be smoking hot. If it's not smoking, it's not hot enough.

## FAQs About Perfect Ribeye Steak

**Can I use a different cut?**

Ribeye is best for this method, but strip steak or porterhouse work too. Avoid lean cuts like sirloin.

**Do I have to flip frequently?**

Yes. Frequent flipping gives more even cooking. Old advice about flipping once is wrong.

**Can I cook this on the grill?**

Absolutely. Same principles apply: high heat and frequent flipping. Skip the butter basting on the grill though.

**What if my steaks are thinner?**

Reduce cooking time. Use a thermometer to check doneness. Thin steaks overcook quickly.

**Why does the steak need to rest?**

Resting allows juices to redistribute. Cut it immediately and juices run out onto the plate.
