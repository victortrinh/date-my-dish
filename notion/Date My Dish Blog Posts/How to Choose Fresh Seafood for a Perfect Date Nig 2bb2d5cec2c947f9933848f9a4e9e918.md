## Why Fresh Matters More with Seafood

Seafood quality declines rapidly after harvest. Unlike beef that improves with aging, fish and shellfish are at their peak immediately and degrade quickly. The difference between day-old and week-old fish is dramatic.

Serving truly fresh seafood shows you understand this and prioritize quality. It's a level of care that translates to other areas.

## Signs of Fresh Fish

### Whole Fish

**Eyes:**

- Clear, bright, slightly bulging (fresh)
- Avoid: Cloudy, sunken, or dull

**Smell:**

- Clean, ocean-like, mildly briny (fresh)
- Avoid: Fishy, ammonia-like, or sour

**Gills:**

- Bright red or pink, moist (fresh)
- Avoid: Brown, gray, or slimy

**Flesh:**

- Firm, springs back when pressed (fresh)
- Avoid: Soft, leaves indentation, separating from bones

**Display:**

- Completely buried in ice, belly cavity filled with ice (fresh)
- Avoid: Sitting on top of ice, warm to touch

---

### Fish Fillets

**Appearance:**

- Translucent, glistening, vibrant color (fresh)
- Avoid: Dull, opaque, brown edges, gaps in flesh

**Moisture:**

- Moist but not sitting in liquid (fresh)
- Avoid: Dry surface or pool of liquid in package

**Texture:**

- Firm, holds together, smooth surface (fresh)
- Avoid: Mushy, flaking apart, slimy feel

**Smell:**

- Mild, fresh ocean scent (fresh)
- Avoid: Strong fishy smell, ammonia, sour

---

## Shellfish Freshness Indicators

### Live Shellfish (Clams, Mussels, Oysters)

**Shells:**

- Tightly closed or close when tapped (fresh)
- Avoid: Open and don't close when handled

**Storage:**

- In mesh bags, refrigerated, not submerged in water (fresh)
- Avoid: In sealed plastic, sitting in water, room temperature

**Weight:**

- Heavy for their size, full of water (fresh)
- Avoid: Light, hollow feeling

**Harvest date:**

- Tagged with harvest date within 7-10 days (fresh)
- Avoid: No tag or old date

---

### Shrimp

**Color:**

- Translucent, gray-blue or pink (species dependent), uniform color (fresh)
- Avoid: Black spots (deterioration), yellowing, dull appearance

**Texture:**

- Firm, intact shells if shell-on (fresh)
- Avoid: Soft, mushy, shells falling off

**Smell:**

- Mild, sweet, ocean-like (fresh)
- Avoid: Ammonia smell (means not fresh)

**Frozen:**

- Most shrimp is previously frozen anyway
- Frozen at sea is often fresher than "fresh" thawed shrimp
- Look for individually quick frozen (IQF)

---

### Scallops

**Appearance:**

- Ivory to pale pink, slightly translucent (fresh)
- Avoid: Bright white (chemically treated), sitting in milky liquid

**Texture:**

- Firm, dry surface (fresh)
- Avoid: Mushy, overly wet

**Dry vs. Wet:**

- "Dry" scallops: untreated, better flavor (preferred)
- "Wet" scallops: treated with solution, won't sear properly (avoid)

Ask specifically for dry scallops. They're worth the higher price.

## Questions to Ask at the Counter

Asking informed questions shows you know what you're doing and gets better service.

**"What came in today?"**

Fish counters receive deliveries on specific days. Today's delivery is freshest.

**"Can I smell this?"**

Any reputable counter will let you smell before buying. Fresh fish should smell clean.

**"Is this dry or wet scallop?"**

Shows you understand scallop processing. They'll respect the knowledge.

**"Was this previously frozen?"**

Not a dealbreaker (most fish is), but you should know so you don't refreeze it.

**"Where is this from?"**

Local or domestic often means fresher supply chain. Also shows interest in sourcing.

## Storage and Handling

### Immediate Storage

**At store:** Ask for extra ice to transport

**At home:**

- Remove from original packaging
- Place on plate, cover with damp paper towel
- Set plate over bowl of ice in coldest part of fridge
- Use within 24 hours

### Before Cooking

**Remove from fridge 10-15 minutes before**

Seafood cooks quickly. You want slight chill, not ice cold.

**Pat completely dry**

Moisture prevents proper searing and browning.

## What to Avoid

**"Fishy" smell**

Fresh fish doesn't smell fishy. If it does, it's old.

**Displayed on ice but not buried in it**

Proper display has fish completely covered with ice.

**No date or source information**

Quality markets provide harvest dates and sourcing details.

**Seafood counter with no customers**

Slow turnover means older product.

**Buying more than 24 hours ahead**

Seafood degrades quickly. Day-of purchase is ideal.

## The Market Timing Strategy

**Best days to buy:**

- Thursday-Friday (weekend delivery)
- Day after delivery day (ask when they receive shipments)

**Times to go:**

- Morning (freshest selection)
- Not Sunday evening (older stock)

**Where to buy:**

- Dedicated fish markets (highest turnover)
- High-end grocery seafood counters
- Avoid pre-packaged in styrofoam when possible

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is frozen seafood bad?**

No. Flash-frozen at sea is often fresher than "fresh" that's been sitting for days. IQF (individually quick frozen) is excellent quality.

**Q: What's the white stuff that comes out of salmon?**

Albumin (harmless protein). Appears when fish is overcooked or cooked too quickly. Brine the salmon first to minimize it.

**Q: How do I know if shellfish is alive?**

Tap the shell. Live ones close. Dead ones stay open and should be discarded.

**Q: Can I buy seafood the day before?**

Yes, but quality declines. If you must, store properly over ice and use within 24 hours max.

**Q: Why does some fish cost so much more?**

Seasonal availability, fishing method, sustainability, and supply chain. Wild-caught and day-boat fish command premium prices for good reason.
