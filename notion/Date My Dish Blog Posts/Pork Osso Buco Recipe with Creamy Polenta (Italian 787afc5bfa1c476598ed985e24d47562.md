## Ingredients for Pork Osso Buco with Polenta

**For the Pork Osso Buco:**

- 2 thick-cut pork shank sections, 2 to 3 inches thick
- Salt and black pepper to taste
- 1 tsp olive oil
- 1 tsp butter
- 2 tbsp diced pancetta or bacon
- 1/3 large yellow onion, diced
- 1 small rib celery, diced
- 1/3 large carrot, diced
- 1/4 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 tsp all-purpose flour
- 1/3 cup white wine (80 ml)
- 1 bay leaf
- Pinch of dried thyme
- Pinch of dried rosemary
- 2/3 cup chicken broth (160 ml), plus more as needed

**For Serving:**

- [Fresh Gremolata Recipe (Classic Italian Herb Garnish)](Fresh%20Gremolata%20Recipe%20(Classic%20Italian%20Herb%20Garni%20d7b28e54d56c49f88f2dd8f319c1492b.md)
- 1 batch [Creamy Parmesan Polenta Recipe (Italian Comfort Food Made Easy)](Creamy%20Parmesan%20Polenta%20Recipe%20(Italian%20Comfort%20Fo%20abcd1e7501d44356a6a2d5b403f03e41.md)

**Ingredient Notes:**

Pork shanks can be found at butcher shops in Montreal like Boucherie Lawrence or Boucherie Viandes Forget. Ask your butcher to cut them 2-3 inches thick.

Pancetta adds authentic Italian flavor, but bacon works great too. You can find pancetta at Italian groceries like Milano.

Use a dry white wine you'd actually drink. Pinot grigio or sauvignon blanc work well.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Sear the Pork Shanks**

Pat the pork shanks dry with paper towels and season generously on all sides with salt and black pepper. Heat the olive oil and butter in a heavy-bottomed pot or Dutch oven over high heat. When the butter is melted and the pan is very hot, add the pork shanks. Sear on all sides until deeply golden brown, about 3 to 4 minutes per side. Don't rush this. The browning creates flavor. Remove the shanks and set aside on a plate.

**Step 2: Cook the Pancetta and Vegetables**

Reduce heat to medium. Add the diced pancetta or bacon to the same pot. Cook until crispy, about 3 to 4 minutes. Add the diced onion, celery, carrot, and 1/4 tsp salt. Cook, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables are softened and starting to caramelize, about 5 to 7 minutes.

**Step 3: Add Tomato Paste and Flour**

Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 2 minutes, stirring constantly. The tomato paste will darken and become more concentrated. Sprinkle in the flour and stir for another 2 minutes. This creates a base that will thicken the sauce.

**Step 4: Deglaze with Wine**

Pour in the white wine and use a wooden spoon to scrape up all the browned bits from the bottom of the pot. This is where tons of flavor lives. Let the wine simmer until it's reduced by about half, about 3 to 4 minutes.

**Step 5: Add Herbs and Broth**

Add the bay leaf, dried thyme, dried rosemary, and chicken broth. Stir to combine and bring to a simmer.

**Step 6: Braise the Pork**

Return the seared pork shanks to the pot, nestling them into the liquid. The liquid should come about halfway up the shanks. If it doesn't, add a bit more broth. Bring to a gentle simmer, then reduce heat to low. Cover the pot and let it braise for 3 to 3 1/2 hours. Check occasionally and add more broth if the liquid is reducing too much. The meat is done when it's fall-apart tender and pulls away from the bone easily.

**Step 7: Make the Gremolata**

While the pork braises, make the [Fresh Gremolata Recipe (Classic Italian Herb Garnish)](Fresh%20Gremolata%20Recipe%20(Classic%20Italian%20Herb%20Garni%20d7b28e54d56c49f88f2dd8f319c1492b.md).

**Step 8: Make the Polenta**

About 50 minutes before the pork is done, make the [Creamy Parmesan Polenta Recipe (Italian Comfort Food Made Easy)](Creamy%20Parmesan%20Polenta%20Recipe%20(Italian%20Comfort%20Fo%20abcd1e7501d44356a6a2d5b403f03e41.md). Time it so the polenta is ready when the pork is done.

**Step 9: Serve**

Spoon the creamy polenta onto plates or shallow bowls. Place a pork shank on top of each portion of polenta. Spoon the braising sauce and vegetables over the pork. Top with a generous spoonful of gremolata. Serve immediately.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Osso Buco

**Don't Skip the Sear**

That golden-brown crust on the pork adds incredible depth to the final dish.

**Low and Slow**

The braise should be at a gentle simmer, not a rolling boil. Low heat breaks down the tough connective tissue.

**Check the Liquid**

If the liquid reduces too much during braising, add more broth. You want enough sauce to spoon over the polenta.

**Make Gremolata Fresh**

Gremolata loses its brightness if made too far ahead. Make it the day of serving.

**Time the Polenta**

Start the polenta about 50 minutes before the pork is done so everything finishes together.

## FAQs About Pork Osso Buco

**Can I use veal shanks instead?**

Yes, the traditional osso buco uses veal. The cooking time and method are the same.

**Can I make this ahead?**

Yes. The braised pork actually gets better the next day. Reheat gently on the stove and make fresh polenta and gremolata when serving.

**What if I don't have a Dutch oven?**

Any heavy-bottomed pot with a lid works. You can also sear on the stovetop and transfer to a covered roasting pan in a 325°F oven.

**My sauce is too thin. How do I thicken it?**

Remove the pork shanks and simmer the sauce uncovered to reduce it. Or make a slurry with 1 tsp cornstarch and 1 tbsp water and stir it in.

**Can I skip the gremolata?**

You can, but don't. The bright, fresh flavors cut through the rich braised pork and make the dish complete.
