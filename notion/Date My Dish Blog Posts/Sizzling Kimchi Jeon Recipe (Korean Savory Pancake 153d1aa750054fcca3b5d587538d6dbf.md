## Ingredients for Kimchi Jeon

**For the Pancake:**

- 125 g all-purpose flour (1 cup)
- 60 g rice flour (1/2 cup)
- 32 g cornstarch (1/4 cup)
- 1/2 tsp salt (3 g)
- 30 ml sesame oil (2 tbsp)
- 120 ml water (1/2 cup)
- 225 g ground pork (8 oz)
- 6 shiitake mushrooms, thinly sliced
- 1 zucchini, cut into half-moons
- 150 g kimchi, chopped (1 cup)
- 100 g garlic chives or green onions, cut into 5 cm pieces (2 cups)
- 1 medium carrot, cut into matchsticks
- 1 red chili, thinly sliced
- 1 shallot, thinly sliced
- Vegetable oil for frying

**For the Dipping Sauce:**

- 60 ml soy sauce (1/4 cup)
- 8 g gochugaru (1 tbsp)
- 15 ml rice vinegar (1 tbsp)
- 3 g sesame seeds (1 tsp)
- 2 green onions, chopped
- 1 garlic clove, minced

**Ingredient Notes:**

Rice flour is essential for getting that crispy texture. Don't skip it or substitute with more all-purpose flour.

Kimchi should be well-fermented for the best flavor. If your kimchi is fresh, it won't have enough tang. You can find good kimchi at any Asian grocery store in Montreal or even at some regular supermarkets now.

Gochugaru is Korean red pepper flakes. It's milder and slightly sweet compared to regular chili flakes. You can find it at Korean grocery stores or order it online.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Make the Batter**

In a large bowl, whisk together the all-purpose flour, rice flour, cornstarch, salt, and sesame oil. Slowly add about 120 ml of water, whisking until you have a loose but thick batter. It should be thicker than crepe batter but thinner than pancake batter.

**Step 2: Add the Pork**

Add the ground pork to the batter and whisk it in until it's evenly distributed. This might seem weird, but mixing the raw pork into the batter ensures it cooks evenly throughout the pancake.

**Step 3: Add the Vegetables**

Fold in all your vegetables: mushrooms, zucchini, kimchi, garlic chives, carrot, red chili, and shallot. Mix everything together so the vegetables are coated in batter. The mixture will be very chunky and packed with ingredients.

**Step 4: Heat Your Pan**

Heat a large non-stick skillet over medium heat. Add a generous amount of oil, enough to coat the bottom of the pan well. You need more oil than you think. This is what creates that crispy exterior.

**Step 5: Cook the First Side**

Scoop about 1 cup of batter into the hot pan and spread it out to about 1/2 inch thick. Don't make it too thin or it'll fall apart. Let it cook undisturbed for 4-5 minutes. You'll know it's ready to flip when the edges are golden and the pancake holds together when you shake the pan.

**Step 6: Flip and Finish**

Carefully flip the pancake with a large spatula. Add a little more oil around the edges if needed. Cook the second side for another 4-5 minutes until golden and crispy. The key is patience. Don't rush this or the inside won't cook through.

**Step 7: Drain and Repeat**

Transfer the cooked pancake to a paper towel to drain excess oil. Repeat with the remaining batter, adding more oil to the pan each time.

**Step 8: Make the Sauce**

While the pancakes cook, mix all the sauce ingredients together in a small bowl. The gochugaru will bloom in the soy sauce and vinegar, creating this beautiful red color.

**Step 9: Serve**

Cut the pancakes into wedges and serve hot with the dipping sauce on the side.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Kimchi Jeon

**Use Enough Oil**

This isn't the time to be stingy with oil. You need a good amount to get that crispy, golden crust. If the pan looks dry, add more.

**Don't Flip Too Early**

Wait until the first side is properly golden before flipping. If you flip too early, the pancake will fall apart.

**Keep the Heat at Medium**

Too high and the outside burns before the inside cooks. Too low and it gets greasy instead of crispy. Medium heat is the sweet spot.

**Chop Your Kimchi**

Large pieces of kimchi make the pancake harder to flip and eat. Chop it into smaller pieces for better distribution.

## FAQs About Kimchi Jeon

**Can I make this vegetarian?**

Absolutely. Skip the pork and add more vegetables, or use crumbled tofu. The recipe notes suggest Beyond Meat for better texture if you want a meat substitute.

**What if I don't have rice flour?**

You really need rice flour for the proper texture. All-purpose flour alone will make it dense and heavy instead of crispy and light.

**Can I make the batter ahead?**

It's best to cook these right after mixing the batter. If the batter sits too long, the vegetables release water and it becomes too loose.

**How do I store leftovers?**

Store in the fridge for up to 2 days. Reheat in a hot skillet with a little oil to crisp them back up. Microwaving makes them soggy.

**What if I can't find garlic chives?**

Regular green onions work perfectly. Use the green parts only, cut into 5 cm pieces.
