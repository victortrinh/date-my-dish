## Ingredients for Fondant Potatoes

- 3 large russet potatoes
- 2 tbsp high-heat vegetable oil (grapeseed or canola)
- Salt and black pepper to taste
- 3 tbsp butter (45 g)
- 4 sprigs fresh thyme, plus more for garnish
- 1/2 cup chicken broth (120 ml), plus more as needed

**Ingredient Notes:**

Russet potatoes are essential. Their high starch content gives you that creamy interior. Yukon golds won't work as well.

Use a high-heat oil like grapeseed, canola, or vegetable oil. Olive oil will smoke at the high temperature you need.

Fresh thyme is important. Dried thyme won't give you the same aromatic quality.

Chicken broth adds flavor as the potatoes steam in the oven. You can use vegetable broth for a vegetarian version.

The butter creates the rich, glossy finish. Don't skip it.

## Step-by-Step Instructions

**Step 1: Preheat the Oven**

Preheat your oven to 425°F (220°C). You want it fully hot before the potatoes go in.

**Step 2: Shape the Potatoes**

Cut off both ends of each russet potato to create flat surfaces. Stand each potato on one end. Using a sharp knife, peel the potato from top to bottom in downward strokes to create a uniform cylinder. You're removing the rounded edges to make a straight-sided cylinder. Cut each cylinder in half crosswise to create 6 potato cylinders about 2 inches long.

**Step 3: Soak the Potatoes**

Place the potato cylinders in a bowl of cold water and let them soak for about 5 minutes. This removes excess starch from the outside, which helps them crisp up better when searing.

**Step 4: Heat the Oil**

Heat the vegetable oil in a heavy oven-proof skillet (cast iron works great) over high heat until it shimmers slightly. The pan needs to be very hot for a good sear.

**Step 5: Sear the First Side**

Pat the potatoes completely dry with paper towels. This is crucial. Any water will cause splattering and prevent browning. Place the potato cylinders in the hot oil with their best-looking flat ends down. Reduce heat to medium-high and pan-fry until deeply golden brown, 5 to 6 minutes. Don't move them around. Let them develop that crust. Season the tops with salt and black pepper.

**Step 6: Sear the Second Side**

Flip the potatoes carefully to show the other flat ends. Sear for another 5 to 6 minutes until equally golden. While they cook, use a paper towel held with tongs to carefully blot excess oil from the skillet.

**Step 7: Add Butter and Thyme**

Add the butter and thyme sprigs to the skillet. As the butter melts, pick up a thyme sprig with your tongs and use it like a brush to paint butter over the tops and sides of the potatoes. This is a classic French technique that adds incredible flavor.

**Step 8: Brown the Butter**

Cook until the butter foams. The foam will be white at first, then turn to a pale tan color. This is browned butter, or beurre noisette, and it adds a nutty, complex flavor. Season with more salt and pepper.

**Step 9: Add Stock and Transfer to Oven**

Pour the chicken stock into the skillet around the potatoes. Don't pour it directly on top or you'll wash away the seasoning. Immediately transfer the skillet to the preheated oven.

**Step 10: Roast Until Tender**

Roast for about 30 minutes, or until the potatoes are tender when pierced with a knife. The insides should be creamy and soft. If they're still firm after 30 minutes, add 1/4 cup more stock and cook for another 10 minutes.

**Step 11: Serve**

Carefully remove the skillet from the oven (remember the handle is hot!). Transfer the potatoes to a serving platter. Spoon the thyme-scented butter remaining in the skillet over the potatoes. Garnish with fresh thyme sprigs. Let them cool for about 5 minutes before serving. This lets the steam settle and makes them easier to eat.

## Expert Tips for Perfect Fondant Potatoes

**Make Uniform Cylinders**

The more uniform your potato cylinders, the more evenly they'll cook. Take your time with the shaping.

**Dry the Potatoes Thoroughly**

This is the most important step for getting a good sear. Pat them completely dry.

**Don't Rush the Sear**

Let each side develop a deep golden crust before flipping. Patience here pays off.

**Use an Oven-Proof Skillet**

Cast iron is ideal because it holds heat well and goes from stovetop to oven seamlessly.

**Test for Doneness**

A knife should slide through the potato easily. If there's resistance, they need more time.

## FAQs About Fondant Potatoes

**Why are they called fondant potatoes?**

Fondant means "melting" in French, referring to the creamy, almost melting texture of the interior.

**Can I make these ahead?**

You can shape and soak the potatoes a few hours ahead, but the searing and roasting should be done just before serving for the best texture.

**What if I don't have an oven-proof skillet?**

Sear the potatoes in a regular skillet, then transfer them to a baking dish before adding the stock and going into the oven.

**Can I use a different type of potato?**

Russets are best because of their high starch content. Yukon golds will work but won't be as fluffy inside.

**My potatoes are browning too fast. What do I do?**

Reduce the heat slightly. You want a deep golden color, not black. Better to take an extra minute or two than to burn them.

**How do I store leftovers?**

Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. Reheat in a 400°F oven for 10-15 minutes to crisp them up again.
