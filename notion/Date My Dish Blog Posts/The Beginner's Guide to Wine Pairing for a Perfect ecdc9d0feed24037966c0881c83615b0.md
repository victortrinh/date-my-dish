## The Core Principle

Wine pairing isn't about memorizing specific combinations. It's about balance.

**Match weight and intensity:** Light foods with light wines, heavy foods with heavy wines.

That's it. Everything else builds from this foundation.

## The Three Simple Rules

### Rule 1: Match Weight

**Light dishes need light wines**

- Salads, fish, chicken
- Pair with: Sauvignon Blanc, Pinot Grigio, light Rosé

**Medium dishes need medium wines**

- Pasta with cream sauce, pork, turkey
- Pair with: Chardonnay, Pinot Noir, medium-bodied reds

**Heavy dishes need heavy wines**

- Steak, lamb, rich stews
- Pair with: Cabernet Sauvignon, Malbec, Syrah

**Why this works:** Balanced intensity means neither wine nor food overwhelms the other.

---

### Rule 2: Acid Cuts Fat

**Rich, fatty foods need acidic wines**

Examples:

- Fatty fish (salmon) + acidic white (Sauvignon Blanc)
- Creamy pasta + high-acid white (Champagne, Pinot Grigio)
- Cheese board + acidic red (Chianti, Sangiovese)

**Why this works:** Acid cleanses the palate, preventing richness from becoming overwhelming.

---

### Rule 3: Consider the Sauce, Not Just the Protein

The sauce determines pairing more than the meat.

Examples:

- Chicken in cream sauce → White wine (Chardonnay)
- Chicken with tomato sauce → Red wine (Chianti)
- Chicken with lemon and herbs → Acidic white (Sauvignon Blanc)

**Why this works:** Sauce is what your palate primarily tastes.

## Wine Characteristics Explained

### Body (Light to Full)

**Light-bodied:**

- Feels delicate, refreshing
- Lower alcohol (under 12.5%)
- Examples: Pinot Grigio, Beaujolais

**Medium-bodied:**

- Balanced, versatile
- Moderate alcohol (12.5-13.5%)
- Examples: Pinot Noir, Merlot, Chardonnay

**Full-bodied:**

- Rich, heavy, coating
- Higher alcohol (13.5%+)
- Examples: Cabernet Sauvignon, Syrah, oaked Chardonnay

---

### Acidity (Low to High)

**Low acid:**

- Smooth, soft
- Can feel flabby with rich food
- Examples: Merlot, Viognier

**High acid:**

- Crisp, bright, mouthwatering
- Cuts through fat
- Examples: Sauvignon Blanc, Chianti, Champagne

---

### Tannin (Red Wines Only)

**What it is:** Compounds from grape skins that create drying sensation

**Low tannin:**

- Smooth, easy-drinking
- Good for lighter foods
- Examples: Pinot Noir, Beaujolais

**High tannin:**

- Grippy, astringent, needs protein
- Great with fatty meats
- Examples: Cabernet Sauvignon, Nebbiolo

**Why it matters:** Tannin binds to protein and fat, softening the wine. High-tannin wines need rich food to taste balanced.

## Pairing by Dish Type

### Seafood and Fish

**Delicate white fish (sole, halibut):**

- Pair: Pinot Grigio, Albariño
- Why: Light body matches delicate flavor

**Richer fish (salmon, tuna):**

- Pair: Chardonnay, Pinot Noir (yes, red can work)
- Why: Oily fish can handle more weight

**Shellfish (shrimp, scallops, oysters):**

- Pair: Champagne, Sauvignon Blanc, Muscadet
- Why: High acid cuts brininess, cleanses palate

---

### Poultry

**Roast chicken (simple preparation):**

- Pair: Chardonnay, Pinot Noir
- Why: Medium body, versatile

**Chicken with cream sauce:**

- Pair: Chardonnay (especially oaked)
- Why: Rich wine for rich sauce

**Chicken with tomato sauce:**

- Pair: Chianti, Sangiovese
- Why: Acid matches tomato acid

**Grilled or BBQ chicken:**

- Pair: Zinfandel, Grenache
- Why: Fruit-forward wines complement char and sweetness

### Red Meat

**Steak (simply prepared):**

- Pair: Cabernet Sauvignon, Malbec
- Why: Tannin cuts fat, full body matches meat

**Steak with peppercorn sauce:**

- Pair: Syrah/Shiraz
- Why: Spicy wine complements peppery sauce

**Braised short ribs:**

- Pair: Cabernet Sauvignon, Bordeaux blend
- Why: Rich wine for rich, slow-cooked meat

**Burgers:**

- Pair: Malbec, Zinfandel
- Why: Fruit-forward, not too serious

---

### Pasta

**Tomato-based (marinara, arrabbiata):**

- Pair: Chianti, Sangiovese, Barbera
- Why: High acid matches tomato acid

**Cream-based (Alfredo, carbonara):**

- Pair: Chardonnay, Pinot Bianco
- Why: Rich wine for rich sauce

**Oil-based (aglio e olio):**

- Pair: Pinot Grigio, Vermentino
- Why: Light, bright, doesn't overwhelm simple flavors

**Pesto:**

- Pair: Vermentino, Sauvignon Blanc
- Why: Herbal notes complement basil

---

### Vegetarian

**Salads:**

- Pair: Sauvignon Blanc, dry Rosé
- Why: Crisp, refreshing, high acid for dressings

**Mushroom dishes:**

- Pair: Pinot Noir, Nebbiolo
- Why: Earthy wines complement earthy mushrooms

**Grilled vegetables:**

- Pair: Grenache, Rosé
- Why: Medium body, fruit complements char

## Common Pairing Mistakes

❌ **Red wine with fish (blanket rule)**

✅ Light reds (Pinot Noir) work beautifully with salmon and tuna

❌ **White wine must be chilled to ice-cold**

✅ Over-chilling mutes flavor. 45-50°F is ideal.

❌ **Red wine at room temperature**

✅ "Room temperature" meant before central heating. Slightly cool (60-65°F) is better.

❌ **Expensive wine is always better**

✅ Quality matters, but $50 isn't twice as good as $25. Diminishing returns above $30-40 for most wines.

❌ **Pairing wine with every ingredient**

✅ Focus on the dominant flavor or sauce, not every component.

## When Pairing Doesn't Matter

**Drink what you like.** If you genuinely prefer red with fish or white with steak, drink it. These are guidelines, not laws.

**When food is the star:** Simple wine lets exceptional food shine

**Casual meals:** Pizza, burgers, tacos don't require perfect pairing

**When you're unsure:** Champagne or Pinot Noir work with almost everything

## The Presentation

### Opening Wine

- Remove foil cleanly
- Insert corkscrew straight down
- Pull smoothly (avoid popping cork)
- Pour small taste first (for you, not date)
- Pour 1/3 to 1/2 glass (allows aroma to develop)

### Discussing Wine

**What to say:**

- "I chose this because it should complement the [dish]"
- "This has nice acidity to cut through the richness"
- "Notice how the wine changes with each bite"

**What not to say:**

- Pretentious tasting notes ("I'm getting hints of tobacco and leather")
- Price (never mention unless they ask)
- "This should go with this" (too prescriptive)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What if my date doesn't drink wine?**

Have alternatives ready. Beer, cocktails, or non-alcoholic options. Don't pressure.

**Q: How much wine should I buy for two people?**

One bottle (750ml) provides 4-5 glasses, perfect for two people over dinner.

**Q: Can I pair wine with spicy food?**

Yes. Off-dry whites (Riesling, Gewürztraminer) work best. Avoid high-alcohol reds (intensifies heat).

**Q: What about dessert wine?**

Optional. Port, Sauternes, or late-harvest Riesling. Or skip wine and have coffee with dessert.

**Q: Should I decant?**

Older reds (10+ years) benefit from decanting. Young wines can too, but not essential for most date night wines.
